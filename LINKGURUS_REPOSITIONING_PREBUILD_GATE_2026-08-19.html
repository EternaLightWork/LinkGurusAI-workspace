02 · Per-Expert Findings

Seven independent reviews

1. 🎯 Strategy & Research Director

Partner-level · Does the corrected thesis follow from the phase research?

Go with changes

✅ What holds up

  • The research repeatedly locates failure at roles, workflows, decision rights, adoption, and operating-model collision rather than at tool functionality alone.
  • T2’s deployment-collision thesis is the cleanest research bridge: the technology works while the organization was not redesigned to receive it.
  • The 6A AI Fit Gate correctly makes AI a “Now / Later / No” consequence of diagnosis rather than a mandatory starting point.
  • The research itself warns that facilitated decision-design was a hypothesis at risk of confirmation bias; the corrected thesis appropriately reduces its strategic altitude.

⚠️ What does not hold

SR-1 · The governing position still defines a management consultancy, not the connection point between organization design and agentic implementation.

The final bundle’s “one competence” is roles, job architecture, decision rights, authority, and accountability. That is a strong OD core, but it does not state the corrected enterprise outcome or end-to-end chain.

Specific fix: Replace the governing strategic choice with a one-sentence category position, a causal thesis, and a delivery boundary. State what Linkgurus owns from strategy through workflow implementation and customer-value realization.
SR-2 · Hypothesis A remains too structurally prominent.

The Lab is still Practice 1, DSA remains a packaging benchmark, and the flagship begins from sprint logic. Facilitation is formally called a mechanism, but the architecture still makes it feel like the firm’s center of gravity.

Specific fix: Reclassify decision-design as the strategy/selection method used across practices—or explicitly redefine The Lab as the strategy-and-value front end of agentic organization work.
SR-3 · Research supports the organizational thesis, not a complete technical implementation claim.

Current research establishes the market problem and the OD/operating-model logic. It does not specify the technical delivery system required to implement agents.

Specific fix: Do not stretch market research into capability evidence. Define the delivery model and technical boundary separately; until then, phrase the technical part as an intended capability, not an established offer.
📍 Evidence cited: SR-1 → S1:17–26, 67–73; SR-2 → S1:81–93, 135–140 and S6:582–597; SR-3 → S7:64–79 and S9:118–131. Research support → S7:41–53; S8:88–137; S6:337–357.

2. 🛠️ Service Design / Innovation Director

Director-level · Can the nine offers deliver strategy → workflows → customer value?

No-go on delivery model

✅ What holds up

  • Entry → flagship → scale creates a legible purchase ladder.
  • The Install has an unusually strong completion condition: authority must move and be exercised in live decisions.
  • The Lab flagship reaches workflow redesign, user testing, a Stop/Keep/Scale decision, and an initial implementation record.
  • T7 forces an owner, live evidence, review date, and reversal/escalation condition onto every offer.

⚠️ What does not hold

SD-1 · T7 is an evidence record, not an implementation service system.

Five fields can document intent and follow-up; they do not define mobilization, delivery governance, dependency management, adoption work, or benefits realization.

Specific fix: Turn T7 into a delivery operating system with stages, roles, cadence, decision forums, dependency log, adoption plan, value measures, acceptance gates, and post-launch stabilization.
SD-2 · The nine offers do not create one continuous service journey.

The Lab decides and tests; Org Design enacts authority; Operating Model maps agents and controls. Handoffs between them are not specified, and there is no single engagement architecture that carries one business outcome through all three.

Specific fix: Create an end-to-end service blueprint with one shared client case, showing entry conditions, outputs, handoffs, client/Linkgurus/partner responsibilities, and exit criteria at every stage.
SD-3 · The agentic flagship ends at an operating-model blueprint.

Its outputs are sequence, changed roles, named owners, and auditable controls. None of the governing scopes requires a working agentic workflow in production or a controlled live pilot.

Specific fix: Either (A) add a live human-agent workflow pilot and stabilization gate to the flagship, or (B) rename it as agentic organization design and stop claiming full implementation.
SD-4 · Scale is review-heavy, not value-optimization-heavy.

Quarterly re-inventory and accountability review are necessary but do not operate or improve agentic workflows.

Specific fix: Expand Scale to include performance review, workflow tuning, role/control updates, value realization, incident learning, and reauthorization of autonomy boundaries.
📍 Evidence cited: SD-1 → S1:101–116; SD-2 → S1:79–93 and S5:314–324; SD-3 → S1:87–89, S5:286–310, S11:126–164; SD-4 → S5:303–324. Strong enactment comparator → S4:583–592.

3. 🏗️ Organizational Design Expert

15+ years · Would a Chief Org Design Officer recognize peer-level OD?

Go with substantial changes

✅ What holds up

  • The authority ladder is genuine OD: job architecture, spans/layers, role charters, decision ownership, approval thresholds, escalation, and enactment.
  • The completion test rejects org-chart approval as success and requires live exercise of authority.
  • The buyer framing correctly treats structure as a political and behavioral system, not a drawing exercise.
  • The 6A sequence has a credible OD logic: baseline, AI-fit choice, authority anchor, entry into work, and hold.

⚠️ What does not hold

OD-1 · “One competence” is narrower than the corrected thesis requires.

The materials cover role/authority architecture well but do not fully specify capability architecture, value streams, governance forums, macro-structure, performance system, workforce transition, or enterprise change architecture.

Specific fix: Publish an explicit Linkgurus Organization Design Method with these design domains, the circumstances in which each applies, and the artifacts/decisions produced.
OD-2 · Organization design is fragmented across practices.

Workflow work sits in The Lab and Operating Model; structural design sits in Org Design; agent controls sit in Operating Model. There is no integrating target operating model that connects strategy, value streams, capabilities, structure, governance, people, process, technology, and measures.

Specific fix: Add one cross-practice Target Operating Model spine and a single integrated design authority that governs trade-offs across all domains.
OD-3 · Change management is asserted more than designed.

The brand line includes application and change management, but the offer specifications do not show change impact analysis, sponsor coalition, transition states, stakeholder commitments, reinforcement, or adoption measures.

Specific fix: Define the change-and-enactment workstream, artifacts, sponsor decisions, and hold measures inside each flagship; do not leave it as T7 copy.
OD-4 · Customer value is not consistently designed back into structure.

Value on Hold begins with value, but The Install’s measures center on authority thresholds. A reorganized enterprise can exercise authority and still fail to improve customer value.

Specific fix: Require every flagship to trace structure and role decisions to a value stream, customer outcome, operational measure, and owner.
📍 Evidence cited: OD-1 → S1:19–24, 84–89 and S4:583–592; OD-2 → S1:67–73, 118–131; OD-3 → S1:73, 101–116 and S4:583–592; OD-4 → S13:108–140 compared with S4:583–592.

4. 🤖 Agentic AI Systems Architect

Hands-on implementation · Is the agentic claim technically defensible?

No-go on current claim

✅ What holds up

  • The Agent-to-Owner Blueprint is more specific than generic AI governance: trigger, inputs/access, action, result, autonomy boundary, prohibited actions, human owner, intervention/stop, escalation, evidence log, success and harm measures.
  • The materials correctly insist on a named human owner and real authority to intervene.
  • The function-by-function entry sequence is a sensible anti-“big bang” principle.
  • The explicit exclusion of security/model-risk assurance is honest—provided the firm does not simultaneously claim end-to-end technical implementation.

⚠️ What does not hold

AI-1 · The claim and the contractual boundary contradict each other.

The corrected thesis says design and implement real agentic organizations. The final bundle says “No technical implementation, security assurance, legal opinion or regulatory assurance” while the flagship only maps/designs.

Specific fix: Choose and document one model: Linkgurus as prime contractor with technical delivery capability; Linkgurus + named engineering partner under joint acceptance; or organizational implementation only with a narrower claim.
AI-2 · The technical lifecycle is missing.

No governing method defines use-case selection, data/system readiness, agent architecture, tool permissions, identity, integrations, orchestration, test/evaluation design, deployment environment, observability, incident response, versioning, rollback, cost/latency, or production acceptance.

Specific fix: Add an Agentic Implementation Method and artifact set covering all lifecycle stages, with accountable owner and acceptance evidence for each.
AI-3 · The AI Fit Gate is organizationally useful but technically incomplete.

Now/Later/No can decide timing, but not whether a workflow is safe, feasible, economically rational, and operable with available data and systems.

Specific fix: Add technical/economic gates: data availability and quality, system-of-record access, integration complexity, autonomy risk, evaluation feasibility, security/privacy, unit economics, and fallback path.
AI-4 · The buyer and delivery RACI omit necessary technical authorities.

COO/CHRO/CIO/risk are mentioned, but there is no explicit role for enterprise architecture, data owner, information security, legal/privacy, model/AI product owner, platform engineering, or operations.

Specific fix: Define a buying-committee and delivery RACI for the flagship, including who approves autonomy, data access, launch, risk acceptance, and rollback.
📍 Evidence cited: AI-1 → S1:88, 131, 139; AI-2 → S11:126–164 and the absence of lifecycle artifacts in S1:79–140; AI-3 → S1:118–131; AI-4 → S1:67–71, 88–89 and S3:499–516.

Devil’s advocate · Would I fund this?

No-go on agentic flagship purchase

Three practices, nine offers, seven territories, T7, and 6A are internally coherent but cognitively expensive. I need to know what future-state company you create and why that matters now.

CEO-3 · The flagship price and output are commercially mismatched.

At $75k–$140k, a buyer may reasonably expect a live pilot or a highly defensible enterprise blueprint. The current wording does not make that distinction clear.

Specific fix: Either include a live workflow pilot and value hold check, or price/name the work as agentic operating-model design and offer implementation separately.
CEO-4 · Delivery capacity is a purchase risk, not a proof objection.

The sources describe founder-heavy delivery and near-full utilization. Implementation requires continuity across strategy, OD, change, technical build, and stabilization.

Specific fix: Define the named delivery team model, partner coverage, concurrency cap, continuity plan, and executive escalation route before selling the flagship.

7. 💰 Commercial / Business-Model Architect

Partner-level · Do pricing and scalability match implementation reality?

No-go on current economics

✅ What holds up

  • Product pricing avoids trapping the firm in founder-day economics.
  • Paid entry offers are real products, not free diagnosis.
  • Scale is gated behind an installed baseline, which prevents selling review before there is anything to review.
  • The sources are unusually honest that prices are anchors, not validated rates.

⚠️ What does not hold

CM-1 · Fixed-scope workshop economics cannot absorb implementation variability.

Agentic implementation depends on data, integrations, security, platform choices, iteration, evaluation, and client readiness. A single 12–16 week band cannot price those unknowns safely without tighter scope.

Specific fix: Use phase-gated commercial units: paid diagnostic; fixed-fee operating-model design; separately scoped pilot/build; stabilization; recurring governance/value optimization. Keep a headline range only after assumptions are explicit.
CM-2 · Entry → Flagship → Scale omits a commercial implementation gate.

The architecture jumps from blueprint flagship to quarterly review. It does not show where build risk is estimated, contracted, accepted, and funded.

Specific fix: Insert an internal Build/Implement gate inside the flagship or between flagship and scale. External 3-tier simplicity may remain, but the SOW and economics must expose the gate.
CM-3 · Recurrence is designed as audit cadence, not operational value.

Review-only recurrence is vulnerable to budget cuts. Ongoing agentic work is more defensible when tied to performance, drift, incidents, new use cases, and value realization.

Specific fix: Price Scale around a defined service level and value loop, not only periodic re-inventory.
CM-4 · Capacity and partner margin are absent from the model.

The current model nearly fills the founder’s year with four flagships before technical implementation is added. Partner fees, QA, insurance/liability, change requests, and stabilization are not reflected.

Specific fix: Build a delivery P&L by offer: roles, effort range, partner cost, gross margin, contingency, concurrency, payment milestones, change control, and risk reserve.
