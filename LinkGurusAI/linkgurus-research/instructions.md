2. **Mark extrapolations `[EXT]` inline** with the inference named. Density is always highest in Budget availability and Recurring revenue, because no Linkgurus transaction at market price exists to calibrate either.
3. **Additive scoring models hide gates.** Any dimension scoring 1–2 that represents a binding constraint (no budget line, no access, no targeting mechanism) behaves like a gate, not a weight. Phase 3.1's multiplicative model catches this; Phases 6.1 and 7.1 do not, and both carry an explicit structural warning saying so. Always restate the warning rather than silently letting a total mislead.
4. **Market access is not a scored dimension anywhere.** MISA local-partner requirement for professional consulting, Etimad prerequisites, and the RHQ rule (min. 15 staff, 3 C-suite; 2026 exception for specialized needs and contracts ≤ SAR 1M) are FACTs that cap what a 1–10-person Egypt-domiciled firm can reach in KSA. Flag it every time it flatters a row.
5. **Negative evidence discipline.** "Confirmed absent" (searched with stated terms, found nothing, e.g. SDG Arabic DQ materials, SDP MENA chapter) is stronger than "absence of search results" (e.g. IAF CPF in Arabic). LinkedIn, closed procurement and word-of-mouth markets were never searchable — never treat absence there as proof.
6. **Vendor-published statistics are directional, not established.** Most agentic-AI demand data (PwC, Deloitte, CrewAI) and all provider client rosters (DesignThinkers, DSA's ALEC "55 → 6 use cases") are self-reported marketing.
7. **Unentered ≠ open.** A market no supplier has entered may have been evaluated and declined. SDG serves Gulf clients from Beirut in English and has not localized; DSA serves the Gulf without Arabic. That is weak evidence localization isn't worth it to the incumbent.

  Key findings carried forward (do not re-derive in Phase 8)

- **Founder credibility and AI leverage are perfectly inverse across all ten Phase 7 spaces.** Every space scoring 5 on AI leverage scores 2 on founder credibility (S3, S4, S9); every space scoring 4 on credibility scores ≤3 on AI leverage (S1, S2, S6, S7, S10). **No space offers both.** This is the most consequential structural finding in the engagement.
- **Only three spaces have a real external clock**, all regulatory or calendar-driven — S1 (Nitaqat Mutawar, statutory, dated), S8 (Vision review calendar), S2 (go-live/escalation). AI creates pressure without deadlines; pressure produces conversations, deadlines produce purchase orders.
- **No recurring-revenue form is evidenced anywhere in the facilitation or decision-design category.** Every RR score of 4 in Phases 6–7 is a category-norm extrapolation; only the Arabic course has a transaction behind it.
- **Hypothesis A ranked 6th of 10 (31/50) and its rank is the least stable in the set**: it rises to joint 3rd (33) if the Arabic requirement proves real, and falls to last (28) if buyers describe a knowledge gap rather than an alignment gap. The **DSA-partnership variant scores identically (31)** — it buys credibility and access at the exact cost of method ownership and IP, and creates dependency on a firm whose train-the-trainer model monetizes the disappearance of the market being entered.
- **The market's dominant sale is capability transfer**, which structurally destroys repeat facilitation demand (DSA's ELM engagement created a permanent Saudi internal-facilitator non-buyer). Anyone entering facilitation enters a market whose leaders monetize its disappearance.
- **Access, not demand, is the binding constraint on the two best-fitting territories** — T1 (MISA/RHQ) and T2 (integrators control the door to live programs). Both gates open through relationships, not messaging.
- Phase 3.2 flagged a **confirmation-bias risk**: five of seven real-job hypotheses converged on "make a politically dangerous decision safe" — the exact pattern this engagement is predisposed to find. Buyer interviews must be designed to *falsify*, not confirm.

  Key learnings & principles

- Flattery is a named failure mode in this engagement. Unsparing assessment is the explicit standard; over-crediting any direction — including the Arabic-native facilitated-decision-design thesis — is to be avoided.
- The Internal and Capability research areas are the least evidenced in the entire brief, making the founder advantage analysis particularly important and particularly under-supported by current data.
- The Arabic-native facilitated-decision-design direction is a candidate thesis only — neither over-credited nor dismissed, subjected to the same rigor as all other directions.
- Producing outputs from public self-description alone (without primary evidence) is a risk to avoid; pausing a deliverable pending founder input is correct behavior, not a failure to deliver.
- "Repositioning" is the wrong frame and should not be used loosely: there is no established market position to reposition from. The work is construction. Firm-level delivery evidence (one engagement under the entity, at a real price, with a nameable client) is the scarcest input in the entire system.
- Never confuse an interesting idea with a viable market. Whitespace is only strategy when the space is empty **and** buyers exist. A vacancy with a plausible economic explanation should be presumed unattractive until a transaction disproves it.
- Never present a novel-sounding option at an inflated score, and never discount an ordinary-sounding one. Score to evidence, then say plainly where the ranking lands — including when it lands somewhere unwelcome.
