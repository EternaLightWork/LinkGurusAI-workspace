## 0. The premise correction, stated once and not re-argued

**FACT (source-internal).** Phase 9 §4.2 wrote all six pillars Arabic-first. §4.2 states of Pillar 2 that it is *"stronger in Arabic than in English"* and of Pillar 3 the same. §4.4 records that the English rendering of the corporate line *"is weaker than the Arabic."* The direction of derivation in the existing record is **Arabic → English**.

**Consequence for this deliverable.** A localization pass on the pillars would be work already done. The literal-translation column below is therefore repurposed as a **back-test**: for each pillar I reconstruct the Arabic an English-first translator would plausibly have produced from the English gloss, and name the specific failure. This is not a strawman exercise — it is the only way to demonstrate that the shipped Arabic is native rather than accidentally native, and it produces the evidence a reviewer will ask for.

**Two things this reveals that a straight translation pass would not.**

1. **Three pillars have no clean Arabic noun at their centre** — accountability, accountable owner, and strategy execution. Phase 9 routed around all three with sentences instead of words, correctly and probably without recording that it had done so. Those are naming decisions, and they are logged in §C below rather than left implicit.
2. **§4.4's case against Tagline Option 2 is partly built on an English-language reading.** It argues «قرارٌ… لا تقرير» down as *"wordplay,"* against the founder's rejection of clever candidates. In Arabic the device is **جناس اشتقاقي** — derivational paronomasia, a classical rhetorical form with Quranic and literary precedent. It reads as *rhetorical seniority*, not as cleverness. The English word "pun" imports a lightness the Arabic does not carry. INFERENCE, high confidence; flagged for native confirmation in §E. This does not reopen D17 — Option 1 is the corporate line — but it materially strengthens the case for Option 2 as The Lab's practice line, which §4.4 itself already floated.

## Pillar 2 — A decision, not a report

| | |
|---|---|
| **1. English original** | *"A decision, not a report."* — P9 §4.2, Pillar 2 |
| **2. Literal translation — DO NOT SHIP** | «قرار، وليس تقريراً» |
| **3. Localized Arabic — SHIP** | **«قرارٌ… لا تقرير»** |
| **4. Register** | **R2 — contemporary business MSA, at its upper edge.** MSA is not a preference here, it is structural: the line exists only inside the root system (ق‑ر‑ر), which is an MSA-level phenomenon. No dialect rendering can carry it. The upper-edge placement is deliberate — the rhetorical device is what makes the line read senior rather than glib, and softening the register would remove the only thing distinguishing it from a slogan. |
| **5. Cultural / idiomatic notes** | **(a) The literal version is nearly identical and still fails.** وليس adds a syllable that breaks the parallelism and converts a construction into a correction. The ellipsis-and-negation form «قرارٌ… لا تقرير» is balanced; «قرار، وليس تقريراً» is a man arguing. **(b) The device has a name and a status.** جناس اشتقاقي is classical Arabic rhetoric with Quranic precedent. This is why §4.4's "it is wordplay" objection is weaker in Arabic than it looks in English — see §0.2. The line therefore reads as a **scope statement** to a procurement reader, not as marketing — which is exactly the altitude Phase 9 wants. **(d) Typographic requirement, not a copy one.** The tanwīn on **قرارٌ** must be printed. ## Pillar 3 — Work gets delegated. Authority does not.

| | |
|---|---|
| **1. English original** | *"Work gets delegated. Authority does not."* — P9 §4.2, Pillar 3. Sourced verbatim from a customer (Wamda, 27 Jul 2026). |
| **2. Literal translation — DO NOT SHIP** | «يتم تفويض العمل. أما السلطة فلا.» |
| **3. Localized Arabic — SHIP** | **«تفويض العمل دون إرساء النموذج المؤسسي»** |
| **4. Register** | **R2 — contemporary business MSA, governance-inflected.** تفويض الصلاحيات is live Saudi corporate-governance vocabulary — it is the phrase on board delegation-of-authority matrices. Using it signals governance literacy to exactly the reader Phase 9 targets, and it cannot be moved down-register without losing that signal. A dialect rendering would read as a consultant who has not sat on the board side of the table. |
| **5. Cultural / idiomatic notes** | **(a) يتم + verbal noun is the single loudest marker of translated Arabic.** It is how translators render an English agentless passive, and native business writing either uses the internal passive (يُفوَّض) or restructures to avoid the problem entirely. The shipped line restructures. **(b) السلطة vs الصلاحية is a real distinction, not a synonym choice.** السلطة is power in the political and sovereign sense — السلطة التنفيذية, السلطة القضائية. الصلاحية is delegated administrative authority within an institution. A literal translator reaches for السلطة because the English word is "authority"; shipping it would make an org-design line read as a statement about political power. **(c) The repetition is the argument.** تفويض appears twice and the second one is the one being withheld — the missing half becomes audible in a way the English cannot reproduce, because English has to switch nouns ("work" / "authority"). §4.2 already records this; the back-test confirms it survives contact with a translator. **(d) Low-risk flag.** تفويض has a devotional sense in religious register (تفويض الأمر إلى الله). Belief is that it does not activate in a sentence containing العمل and الصلاحية. **UNKNOWN — do not assert; §E.** |

To a Saudi executive that reads as under-deferential. This is one of the few places where an Egyptian-marked habit would surface in otherwise-MSA copy, and it is worth writing into the style guide explicitly. The offer-name family already leans this way (فحص الصلاحيات الدوري), so the association may be an asset rather than a defect — but it is a choice and it has not been made consciously. §E. |

## Cross-pillar findings

**Finding 1 — The failures cluster in one place, and it is not grammar.** Five of the six literal renderings are grammatically acceptable Arabic. They fail on **noun selection** (غرفة, مالك, السلطة, الآلة, حالة تشغيلية) and on **two syntactic tells** (يتم + verbal noun; بما في ذلك). A reviewer checking for grammatical correctness will pass all of them. This is the argument for native *strategic* review rather than proofreading, and it is the practical reason the literal column has to stay in the document.

**Finding 2 — The Arabic is doing positioning work the English cannot reach.** Three pillars carry meaning in Arabic that has no English equivalent: the ق‑ر‑ر root pun (P2), the repeated تفويض with an audible missing half (P3), and المؤسسي binding the spine to the category label (P5). **INFERENCE, high confidence:** an English-language reviewer assessing this system will systematically under-rate it, because the strongest third of it is invisible to them. This is worth stating to anyone brought in to review the bilingual profile.

**Finding 3 — Three pillars have no noun at their centre.** Accountability (P4), accountable owner (P4), strategy execution (P5). Phase 9 solved all three with sentences. That is the correct solution and it is also an unrecorded naming decision, now logged in §C.

**Finding 4 — One tone-of-voice adjective is mistranslated in Phase 9 §4.1.** «مباشر» is given for **Plain**. مباشر means *direct*, and directness in Gulf business communication is not an unambiguously positive trait — it sits close to bluntness and to insufficient deference. The intended quality is *uncomplicated*, which is **واضح** or **بلا تعقيد**. Conversely **«رصين»** for **Senior** is stronger than its English source: رصانة is a named Arabic virtue of composed gravitas with no English single-word equivalent. Recommend: change مباشر → واضح; keep رصين and note that the Arabic tone-of-voice list is the better of the two. INFERENCE, high confidence. §E.

# PART 2 — OUTLINE

## A. The Arabic tagline

**A.1 It already exists, and the brief's framing inverts the actual situation.** «مَن يقرر… وماذا يبقى» was founder-selected at **D17** and confirmed as the corporate line at **D21**, with إرساء النموذج المؤسسي's hook reassigned to «حدود القرار» to clear the double-assignment. It is Arabic-native by construction, not a translation — §4.4 records that the *English* rendering is the weaker one. **Producing a competing Arabic tagline here would overwrite a closed founder decision, which D9 forbids.** What follows is certification against the brief's test, plus the residual risks, plus the English derivative.

**A.2 Certification — four tests.**

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Arabic-native, not translated | **Pass.** No calque, no يتم, no imported metaphor. The ellipsis-and-pair structure is native rhythm. |
| Dialect-neutral | **Pass, and this is the finding.** Identical in MSA, Khaleeji and Egyptian. A Saudi buyer cannot place the speaker's nationality from it — an asset for an Egypt-domiciled firm selling into KSA (Block B11). Neither Phase 9 nor Phase 8.2 records this property; it is worth protecting deliberately in every derivative line. |
| Covers the portfolio | **Pass.** مَن يقرر = المختبر + البنية والتصميم المؤسسي; وماذا يبقى = the T7 spine + نموذج التشغيل الذكي. §4.4 already establishes this. |
| Survives the register requirement | **Pass.** R3, sayable aloud, no wordplay, no superlative, no claim requiring evidence the firm does not have (Block G29). |

**A.3 Two unresolved mechanics — decisions, not defects.**

1. **Is it a question?** The line is interrogative in form but is used as a statement of scope. Arabic typography would ordinarily take a **؟**. Adding it makes the line challenge the reader; omitting it makes it a subject heading. **Recommend omitting the question mark and keeping the ellipsis** — the challenge is implied and stays face-preserving, which is house grammar (adjective 4). Founder call.
2. **Vowel the مَن.** Unvoweled, من is ambiguous between *who* (مَن) and *from* (مِن). In a wordmark lockup rendered large, this is a real legibility issue, not a pedantic one. **The fatha must be set.** Build constraint, same class as §5.6.

**A.4 English derivative.** The English is downstream and should be treated as such — it is a gloss for the company profile, not a parallel tagline. *"Who decides — and what holds."* is the closest available and §4.4 already notes it is weaker. Do not attempt to strengthen the English at the cost of the Arabic; the site is Arabic-first (D16, greenfield).

## D. Register recommendation

**D.1 A correction to the options as framed.** The brief offers MSA vs. Egyptian vs. Gulf/Khaleeji vs. neutral pan-Arab. **A written Khaleeji business register does not meaningfully exist.** Gulf B2B writing — proposals, RFPs, board papers, corporate sites, LinkedIn articles — is MSA. Khaleeji appears in speech, WhatsApp, and short-form video. So the real decision is two decisions, not one:

1. **Which MSA** for everything written (this is where the actual choice lives), and
2. **Whether a spoken layer exists at all**, and in what dialect.

INFERENCE, high confidence, flagged for native confirmation (§E).

**D.2 Recommendation.**

> **Default to contemporary business MSA (R2) across every written surface, including LinkedIn. Use Khaleeji-inflected spoken register in video and voice only. Ship no written Egyptian dialect anywhere.**

**D.3 The four options, with trade-offs stated plainly.**

| Option | What it buys | What it costs | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **High / literary MSA (R1)** | Maximum gravitas. The register of إحكام, رسوخ, of §3.1's naming candidates. | Reads remote and slightly archaic. **It contradicts the founder's own selected voice** — tone-of-voice adjective 3 requires the symptom in the buyer's words, and P4.1 §5 is explicit that *"marketing that opens with operating-model maturity speaks consultant."* R1 is the register of speaking consultant. | **Reject as default.** Retain for one use only: the T7 spine line (Pillar 5), where maxim register is the point. |
| **Contemporary business MSA (R2) — pan-Arab professional** | Correct in Riyadh, Cairo, Dubai and Amman simultaneously. Procurement-legible — it survives an RFP portal, which Set A/Set B names must (§5.3 test 6). Nationality-neutral, which matters for an Egypt-domiciled firm selling into KSA. **And it is the register the entire verbal core is already written in.** | Warmth has to come from somewhere else. It will not, on its own, feel personal. | **Recommend as default.** |
| **Khaleeji spoken register** | Warmth and proximity in video and voice. Signals the seller has spent time in the room, not just in the market. | **A written version has no real precedent in B2B and would read as a stunt.** Spoken, it also carries an authenticity risk: an Egyptian speaker performing Khaleeji is heard immediately, and Block B11 describes a buyer who *"respects you if you take your confident own space"* — a performed accent works against exactly that. | **Adopt in speech only, and unforced.** Khaleeji *vocabulary* in an MSA frame, not accent imitation. |
| **Egyptian register on social** | Genuine warmth, genuinely hers, zero performance cost. | **Two costs, and both compound.** It marks the seller as Egyptian to a Saudi buyer at the moment of first contact, and it marks the content as personal rather than institutional — against D12, where the comparison set is *another consulting firm*. It also bets the LinkedIn channel: the 18,000 followers are a FACT (Block G30) but **their geographic composition is UNKNOWN**, so this is an unhedged wager on the audience being Egyptian-weighted. | **Reject for Linkgurus corporate.** It remains legitimate for the personal brand and the Arabic OD course, which are explicitly not Linkgurus. |

**D.4 The warmth problem, and how the system already solves it.** The usual reason to reach for dialect is that MSA feels cold. This architecture has two better instruments and is already using both:

- **Morphological courtesy inside MSA** — the ‑كم plural address in Pillar 6 (فريقكم). Warmth without leaving the register. This should be a style-guide rule, not an accident.
- **First person and founder-anchoring (D14)** — «نصمّم», «معنا», and the founder's name in the first clause of the boilerplate. **Warmth comes from the person, not from the dialect.** That is what C2 is for, and it is available at no register cost.

**D.5 The constraint that decides this, and it is not linguistic.** `instructions.md` records **G28** — the question on boardroom-standard Arabic and English — as *answered "no", meaning unclear*. That is an open ambiguity about whether boardroom-standard bilingual capability or collateral exists. **A two-register system needs an owner who maintains it**, and no such owner is evidenced. A single default register is recommended partly because it is the only one that does not require an editor the firm has not budgeted for. If G28 resolves favourably, revisit; until then, one register.
