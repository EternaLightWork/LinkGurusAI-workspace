# Linkgurus — Strategic Asset Audit (v2)
## Due-diligence classification, restructured by ownership pool

**What changed from v1.** Assets are now separated into three ownership pools before classification, because v1 presented founder capability, personal-brand assets, and firm assets in one undifferentiated table. That structure obscured the central fact of this situation: Linkgurus is a pre-revenue entity being built, not an established firm being repositioned. The classifications themselves are largely unchanged; the reading of them changes considerably.

**The three pools.**

- **Pool A — Founder capability and experience.** Held by Kholoud Rashdan personally. Not owned by any entity, not transferable by sale, and in several cases evidenced only inside prior employment. This pool is the raw material.
- **Pool B — Personal-brand assets.** Owned by the founder, commercially active, and currently carrying all market signal: the Arabic course, playbook, audience, and published thesis.
- **Pool C — Firm assets (Linkgurus).** Anything the legal entity itself owns or has delivered.

**The structural finding, stated plainly.** Pool C is close to empty, and most of what sits in it classifies DEPRECATE. This is the expected condition of a company being started, not evidence of failure. It does mean that phrases like "rebuilding Linkgurus" and "repositioning Linkgurus" describe different activities: there is no established market position to reposition from. The work is construction, and the audit should be read as an inventory of construction materials rather than as a valuation of a going concern.

**Evidence base.** Phase 1 Strategic Research Brief; founder responses to Blocks A–G; the 6A's artifact; founder clarification on intended AI direction; public seed facts.

**Evidence still absent.** Written case studies; testimonials; formal service list with prices; contracts or invoices; procedure and provenance of the three mapping tools; absolute course figures; audience composition data; GISCON outcome.

## Reading notes

**On "rebuilding from scratch."** The audit supports that description and the pool structure makes it explicit. Pool C contains one functional asset — the legal entity — and everything else in it is either a liability to remove or a gap to fill. This is not a criticism of the plan; it is the plan, correctly inventoried. The practical consequence is that firm-level evidence (a delivered engagement under the entity, at a real price, with a client willing to be named) is the scarcest input in the entire system, and it gates enterprise sales regardless of how strong Pools A and B become.

**On the canvas correction.** The v1 row was wrong in what it named. Adopting and translating third-party canvases is a sourcing decision that produces no defensible asset; building an original instrument informed by those tools produces one. Both rows now appear, classified differently, because they are different things. The licence positions differ materially between the three sources and should be confirmed individually before any build work starts.

**On the AI direction generally.** Every row in Pool D is BUILD or UNKNOWN. That is the honest state of a direction with one delivered antecedent and no sold instance — and it is normal for a direction being entered deliberately. The risk is not that the direction is wrong; it is that trend-driven demand and funded demand are different things, and Phase 1 found the organizational-AI-readiness claim already crowded in MENA. The evidence bar you set for every other direction applies here unchanged.

**The three resolutions that would reclassify the most rows.** Provenance and procedure of the three mapping tools; the GISCON outcome quantified with client confirmation; audience composition data against intended buyer profile.
