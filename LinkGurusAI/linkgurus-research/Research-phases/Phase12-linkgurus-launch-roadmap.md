# 1. Flagship offer packaging

## 1.1 The lead flagship — recommendation

**Recommend: إرساء النموذج المؤسسي (*The Install*) as the launch flagship**, entered through **خارطة الصلاحيات** (*Where Decisions Stop*).

| | |
|---|---|
| **Practice** | البنية والتصميم المؤسسي / Restructuring & Org. Design (T1 + T4) |
| **Buyer** | CEO / owner / board (P8.2 Round 1) |
| **Hook** | «حدود القرار» (D21) |
| **Entry** | خارطة الصلاحيات — 1 week, $9,000–12,000 |
| **Flagship** | إرساء النموذج المؤسسي — 10–14 weeks, $55,000–90,000 |
| **Tail** | فحص الصلاحيات الدوري — 2–3 days, semi-annual, $12,000–18,000 / cycle |

## 1.2 Why this one leads, on the evidence

- **It is the only practice whose three offers form a ladder on one root** — خارطة الصلاحيات → إرساء النموذج المؤسسي → فحص الصلاحيات الدوري (map authority → delegate it → inspect it). P8.2 Round 4 records that the founder built this family herself from her own picks, and that no other practice is this coherent. A buyer can follow it without being taught it. **FACT (source-internal).**
- **Founder credibility is at its cap here.** P6.1 row C scores founder capability **4** ("Geidea was literally this situation"), against **2** for the Practice 3 flagship's territory (row E, zero delivery evidence in category). The cap is 4 by construction — 4 means deepest evidenced competence relative to other rows, still employer-scoped and unverified by a third party. **FACT.**
- **The Arabic is already live in the market.** P4.1 confirms تأخر القرارات · غموض المسؤوليات · عدم وضوح السلطات recur in Arabic business content, and تفويض الصلاحيات is on Saudi board delegation-of-authority matrices (P10 Pillar 3 register note). This practice requires **no category education** — see the contrast in §5, risk 5.
- **It carries the customer's own symptom register.** «قرارٌ يحتاج يوماً… يأخذ أسبوعاً» is customer-produced language (P4.1 §5, Wamda 27 Jul 2026), not supply-side vocabulary.

## 1.3 What this choice costs, stated plainly

**Round 5 selected مجلس القرار as the first *sale*, not as the launch flagship.** These are different objects and both stand. The cost of leading with إرساء النموذج المؤسسي is that **the content engine does not point at it.** Two years of AI content and the ~18,000-follower audience sit on top of decision problems, not job architecture (P8.2 Round 5). The resolution is the two-engine structure already in the record:

| Engine | Sells | Launch role |
|---|---|---|
| **Network** (5 GCC contacts, Geidea/ARASCO alumni) | خارطة الصلاحيات → **إرساء النموذج المؤسسي** | **The flagship.** Sold in a conversation, not a funnel. |
| **Content** (LinkedIn, 2,500 list) | مجلس القرار → مختبر القرار | **The proof and cashflow engine.** Fastest route to a nameable outcome. |
| **Position only** | نموذج تشغيل وكلاء الذكاء الاصطناعي | Claimed in content from day one; not sold until §5 risk 3 clears. |

**The interdependency to hold consciously:** if proof is generated in Practice 1 and the flagship sits in Practice 2, the launch flagship is unproven at launch. §2 resolves this by requiring one of the three proof points to sit inside Practice 2's own ladder.

## 1.4 Delivery model — hybrid, and specifically which hybrid

Not a workshop business, not a retainer business, not pure project delivery. The shape implied by T1+T4 and by the T7 spine is:

1. **Fixed-scope diagnostic** (خارطة الصلاحيات, 1 week) — priced to remove the decision, not to earn (P8.2 Round 3 principle 2). Sold standalone and honoured standalone; the buyer is not obliged to continue.
2. **Fixed-price project with facilitation inside it** (إرساء النموذج المؤسسي, 10–14 weeks) — job architecture, spans and layers, role definition, decision rights, authority thresholds. Facilitated sessions are a *delivery mechanism* inside the project, never the product. Quoting facilitation days re-enters the $600–1,800/day tier the whole repositioning exists to leave (P2.1 §Q; P5.2).
3. **Enactment phase, contractually inside the flagship, not after it** — this is where T7 lives. P3.2 seg 2's confirm/kill test for this exact segment is whether the structure was *enacted* (rights actually moved) or *presented* (deck to board, then drift). The SOW's completion condition must be the operating state, not the approved design. This is a contract-drafting instruction, not a messaging one.
4. **Recurring tail** (فحص الصلاحيات الدوري, semi-annual) — the firm keeps the review cycle. **[EXT]** This is designed, not inherited: P2.2 row 13 RR-2 and P7.1 reading note 4 both record that **no recurring-revenue form is evidenced anywhere in these categories.**

**Scope exclusion is a launch asset, not a gap.** Total rewards and salary-scale design are out of scope (Block B10, founder-declared absent; P1.1 DEPRECATE). Restructuring buyers will ask. Writing the exclusion into the published scope is the credibility move.

## 1.5 Price — and where the evidence runs out

**Recommended range: $55,000–90,000** (SAR 205,000–340,000 at the 3.75 peg), unchanged from P8.2 Round 3. It is defensible, but the defence rests on an *alternative-cost* argument, not on competitor pricing evidence.

**What pricing evidence actually exists (FACT, from P5.1 and P2.1 §Q):**

| Signal | Value | What it bounds |
|---|---|---|
| GCC independent consultant day rates | $600–1,800/day | The tier P5.2 places Linkgurus in today — the floor to escape |
| AI-native boutique engagements (Bosio, P2.1) | $75k–$500k | Boutique band; Practice 3 flagship enters its lower third |

**What does not exist — stated rather than filled in.** **No GCC competitor publishes a price for organization design, restructuring or job-architecture work.** P5.1's own evidence key says so: *"Pricing cells are signals, not rate cards — most firms in this market do not publish prices."* ES Facilitation: **not disclosed.** Orgvue: **undisclosed.** Bakkah, RMG: training-market price points, a different product. Korn Ferry and Mercer: project fees, unpublished. **There is no competitor price point for إرساء النموذج المؤسسي anywhere in the evidence base, and no number should be invented to stand in for one. UNKNOWN.**

**What the range rests on instead, in order of strength:**

1. **The buyer's real alternative is a senior hire.** P3.2 seg 2 records that this buyer benchmarks the spend against hiring a strong COO or fractional CHRO. A GCC COO runs roughly $150k–250k fully loaded per year **[EXT — category norm, no source in the evidence base quotes a GCC COO package directly]**, and a wrong one costs that plus the damage and the delay. Against that, $55–90k for a structure that works is a comparison the CEO makes unprompted.
2. **D12 (founder decision):** the majority of buyers compare Linkgurus to another consulting firm, not to a COO salary and not to a day rate. If D12 holds, this range is **conservative, and the pricing risk sits on the downside** — i.e. under-charging, which is the founder's own diagnosed historical error (Block E21).
3. **No discounts at any tier (D11).** The range is the range.

**Two open inputs only the founder can close, both of which move the number:**

- **The D11 / D13 contradiction.** D11 rejects any founding-client discount mechanism in full; D13 selects Egypt as a case-study ground with work *"priced as founding-client work, not listed."* These are recorded in the same round and are not consistent. §2 needs a resolution: either the first Egypt case-study engagement is at GCC list price, or founding-client terms exist and D11 is narrower than stated. **This blocks nothing until proof point 3 is scheduled — but it blocks that.**
- **Cash runway and pipeline.** Pipeline is zero (Block E21 — FACT as of the record). Runway appears nowhere in Phases 0–11 — **UNKNOWN.** A firm with 18 months of runway can hold the $55–90k anchor through a slow first quarter; a firm with 4 months cannot, and will discount under pressure, which destroys the anchor permanently. **This roadmap assumes the anchor can be held. If it cannot, say so before launch, not during the first negotiation.**

# 2. Proof-before-scale sequencing

## 2.1 What "proof" has to mean for *this* territory

Generic case studies do not work here, because the practice's own thesis names the failure mode it must not exhibit. P3.2 seg 2's confirm/kill test is **enactment vs presentation.** Therefore:

**Proof is evidence that authority moved — not evidence that a design was approved.**

Concretely, a credible proof point for إرساء النموذج المؤسسي contains at least three of:

- A **named authority threshold that changed** (e.g. a spend or hiring decision that previously required the CEO and no longer does), stated as a before/after.
- **Decision latency on 2–3 traced decisions**, measured at the entry offer and re-measured at close. خارطة الصلاحيات is the measurement instrument — it produces the baseline. This is the strongest structural property of the ladder and it is currently unused: **run the entry offer as a baseline even when the flagship is sold directly.**
- A **role that is now held by someone other than the founder or CEO**, named by title.
- **Client willingness to be named.** P1.1 records firm-level delivery evidence — one engagement under the entity, at a real price, with a nameable client — as **the scarcest input in the entire audit.** Willingness must be asked for in the SOW at signature, not requested at close.

**What does not count as proof, and must not be presented as such:** an approved org chart, a board-endorsed design, a satisfaction quote, participant counts, or an unnamed "a GCC financial services company" attribution used as the primary claim. P11 §5 already forbids implied social proof in the visual system for exactly this reason; the rule applies to the sales conversation too.

## 2.2 The three proof points, in dependency order

**Proof point 0 — free, and available now: quantify the GISCON outcome.**
Open evidence gate 3. It is the only named reference engagement and **its outcome is blank** — the single most valuable missing data point in the audit, and it moves founder credibility on four rows simultaneously. Cost: one conversation. **This should be closed before the first proposal goes out, not before launch.** If the outcome cannot be quantified or the client will not confirm it, that is itself information and it should be recorded as closed-negative rather than left open indefinitely.

**Proof point 1 — one مجلس القرار delivered, content-sourced.** ($6–8k, 1 working day)
- **Why first:** lowest friction in the portfolio, four delivered precedents (Block D15), one unprompted client request (P1.2 §1.10, n=1 — self-reported), and it produces a nameable outcome in one day of delivery. With discounting off the table (D11), speed to first case study is the only lever left.
- **What it proves:** that GCC buyers purchase decision work standalone rather than inside an engagement they already bought — the largest open assumption in the research base (P2.2 row 13 MD-3; P5.2 W1, status *"cannot be determined"*).
- **What it does not prove:** anything about the launch flagship. Hold this distinction.

**Proof point 2 — one خارطة الصلاحيات delivered through the network.** ($9–12k, 1 week)
- **Why this is non-negotiable:** it is the only proof point that sits inside the flagship's own ladder. Without it the launch flagship goes public with proof from a different practice. Sold to whichever of the five GCC contacts describes a structure problem rather than a decision problem.
- **What it proves:** the entry offer's core act — the diagnostic that ends an argument by sequencing the consequence (Block C12; P1.2 §1.4 identifies it as a real diagnostic act with an identifiable tell). And it produces the latency baseline that makes proof point 3 measurable.

**Proof point 3 — one founder-led إرساء النموذج المؤسسي, end to end, including enactment.** (10–14 weeks)
- **Where:** Egypt is the designated ground (D13) — a case-study geography, not a listed market. This is the highest-value use of the home market and it converts the price weakness into the evidence engine.
- **Blocked on:** the D11/D13 pricing contradiction (§1.5). Resolve before scheduling.
- **What it proves:** the whole thesis, including the T7 spine — «لا يكتمل التصميم المؤسسي إلا بالتطبيق وإدارة التغيير». Without this, the spine is a claim.
- **Bandwidth warning:** this is ~40–50 founder-days. It cannot run concurrently with a second flagship and a launch. See §5 risk 4.

## 2.3 The falsification discipline that runs alongside

The five GCC conversations double as the 8–10 structured buyer interviews open since P2.1 Rec. 1 (P8.2 Round 5). **P3.2 flagged a confirmation-bias risk explicitly: five of seven real-job hypotheses converged on "make a politically dangerous decision safe" — the exact pattern this engagement is predisposed to find.** These conversations must be built to disconfirm.

The discriminating question is fixed in advance: **do buyers describe a *knowledge* gap ("we don't know how") or an *alignment* gap ("we know, we can't agree")?** Record the answer per conversation before interpreting any of them. Knowledge gap invalidates a large part of the decision-design thesis; alignment gap supports it.

**Two sequencing rules that bind across stages.**

1. **No public claim precedes its verification.** The enterprise-transformation story is the test case: it is the strongest available credibility bridge into the AI direction (P1.1, REPACKAGE) *and* it rests on a scope that is **UNVERIFIED**. Shipping it in Stage 0 would fill it with claims; shipping it in Stage 1 fills it with the thinking; shipping the case studies in Stage 2 fills it with evidence.

# 4. Existing-client and network transition

## 4.1 The structural fact that makes this easier than a normal repositioning

**D14 kept the name.** There is no name-change announcement to make, no domain migration to explain, no "formerly known as." Add the second fact — the legacy site never existed as a real property — and **the usual instability signals of a repositioning are structurally absent here.** The transition risk in this launch is not "the firm changed"; it is "the firm is suddenly visible after being invisible," which is a different and smaller problem.

**The corollary, which is the actual risk:** there is very little to transition *from*. Two lifetime clients, three months each, both at minimum charge, both of whom **bought Kholoud rather than the firm** (Block A1 — FACT). Five GCC contacts, zero approached. An 18,000-follower audience of **unverified composition** that has never been promoted to. This is not a client base being carried across a repositioning — it is a network being activated for the first time.

## 4.2 Language guidance — what to say, and what not to

**The governing grammar is already founder-selected.** Round 4 evidence: every elliptical or clever candidate was rejected; every clear, senior-register candidate was kept. The Arabic voice is precise and senior, never playful (P8.2 Round 4). The transition language must obey the same rule.

**Words that must not appear in any client or network message:**

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| "Rebrand," "relaunch," "new direction," "new chapter," "pivot," "exciting news" | Each announces change for its own sake. To a client, change in a supplier is a risk signal before it is anything else. |
| «إعادة الهيكلة» in any present or future tense | Round 4 rule. P4.1 §4: Arabic press describes restructuring as producing «حالة من القلق الشديد». The only permitted use is the buyer's completed past tense, which the founder herself constructed: «انتهيت من إعادة الهيكلة… المراجعة الدورية معنا». |
| التحول الرقمي · التمكين · الابتكار · ورش العمل | On every Saudi provider site (P5.1 §5). Using them makes the message indistinguishable from the market it is trying to leave. |
| Any reference to the old service lines or "Empowering human excellence" | Void, not deprecated (Phase 9 record). Naming them to disown them re-establishes them. |
| Price explanations, unprompted | A supplier who explains a price before being asked has already conceded it. |

**The frame to use instead: continuity of competence, newly named.** Not "we changed" — "we named what we do."

**Three proposed lines, flagged as proposals.** *Arabic phrasing is the founder's call — these are drafts for her arbitration, not recommendations to ship. Each is checked for a present subject and no stacked modifiers, per the standing note.*

1. **To a past client, opening (Arabic):** «ما نعمله لم يتغيّر. ما تغيّر هو أننا سمّيناه.»
   *What we do has not changed. What changed is that we named it.* — Two short sentences, subject present in both, no imported metaphor.
2. **To a network contact, opening (Arabic):** «سؤالي واحد: أي قرار عندكم يُعاد فتحه أكثر من مرة؟»
   *One question: which decision at your end keeps getting re-opened?* — Plural courtesy (عندكم) per the Gulf-address note (P10 Pillar 6, note d); an Egyptian-marked singular عندك would read as under-deferential to a Saudi executive. This is a question, not a pitch, and it doubles as the falsification interview.
3. **English, for the profile and the signature block:** *"Linkgurus is a management consultancy led by Kholoud Rashdan, working with chief executives and boards across Saudi Arabia and the Gulf on a single question: who decides — and what holds."* — Already built (P10 B.4); use it verbatim rather than paraphrasing it.

## 4.3 Handling the four situations that actually arise

**(a) The two past clients, both at minimum charge.**
Do not re-quote them on the old relationship, and do not explain the price difference. Re-enter through a different door — an entry offer at list price, with a specific observation about their situation — or do not re-enter. **INFERENCE:** a 5–10× price move announced to a former minimum-charge client reads as opportunism; the same price attached to a differently-named product with a defined artefact reads as a different purchase. The KSA client is the more valuable of the two and should be approached with a named observation, not an update.

**(b) The person-to-firm question — the one that genuinely can signal instability.**
The audience follows Kholoud; the entity holds the contracting. If the personal voice suddenly speaks as a company, the network reads a disappearance. **The founder-anchored decision (D14) is the answer, and it has to be visible in the mechanics:** she remains the author of every post; Linkgurus appears as the entity she leads, named in the first clause of the boilerplate (P10 B.3 — placed there deliberately because C2's logic is that the person is the credibility source). No corporate LinkedIn page posting in parallel with hers during Stages 0–2.

**(c) The wordmark problem, in Arabic conversation.**
«لينك جوروز» carries no meaning in Arabic (P9 §1.2). In any Arabic message where the name appears first, the corporate line must follow immediately — this is the same binding rule as the site lockup, applied to correspondence. The name never travels alone in Arabic.

**(d) The Egypt/GCC asymmetry.**
Egypt is a delivery and case-study ground, not a listed market (D13). **Nothing about this is said to anyone.** Two price lists become visible the moment they are explained. The location line stays in the contact block and is not a strategy statement (closed decision — not to be reopened).

# 6. Early success signals — first 90 days

**Framing carried from Phase 0.** Every signal below is stated as an assertion with a **validating** and an **invalidating** observation, and with the consequence named. A signal with no invalidating condition is not a signal; it is a hope. The discipline that governed the research governs the execution.

**A standing warning.** P3.2 recorded that five of seven real-job hypotheses converged on the analyst's own prior. The same bias operates on early launch data, more strongly, because the founder now has money and identity attached to the answer. **Write the thresholds down before the data arrives.**

## 6.1 The signals

**Signal 1 — Audience composition (the fork).**
- **Validates:** replies and enquiries from titled decision-makers (CEO, COO, owner, board) in response to the single list promotion.
- **Invalidates:** replies dominated by HR practitioners, trainers, and individuals asking about courses.
- **Consequence if invalidated:** the content engine is a slow path, not a fast one; the sequence inverts to network-first. This is the **fork that could invalidate the entire 90-day plan** and it is testable in week one for the cost of one email. Test it first.

**Signal 2 — Which gap buyers describe.**
- **Validates the decision-design thesis:** buyers describe an **alignment** gap — "we know what to do, we can't get agreement," "it keeps coming back to the table."
- **Invalidates it:** buyers describe a **knowledge** gap — "we don't know how to do this," "we need someone who has done it."
- **Consequence if invalidated:** Hypothesis A falls to last in the Phase 7 ranking (28/50 from 31), and The Lab's flagship weakens relative to Practice 2 — which strengthens, rather than undermines, the Section 1 recommendation. **This is the deliberately unwelcome outcome and it should be recorded as-is if it occurs.**

**Signal 3 — Standalone purchase of decision work.**
- **Validates:** one مجلس القرار sold on its own, not as a preliminary to a larger engagement the buyer had already committed to.
- **Invalidates:** every interested buyer wants it bundled inside a bigger scope, or wants it free as a scoping call.
- **Consequence if invalidated:** the whole entry-offer architecture is affected, not just one offer — all three entry offers rest on the same assumption that a bounded diagnostic is a purchasable object. P5.2 W1 records this as *"cannot be determined"* from research; only a transaction resolves it.

**Signal 4 — Network warmth converts, or does not.**
- **Validates:** ≥2 of the five GCC contacts convert to a substantive scoping conversation about their own organisation, and ≥1 introduces a third party unprompted.
- **Invalidates:** polite responses, no meetings, no introductions.
- **Consequence if invalidated:** accessibility scores across three directions were built on a hypothesis about goodwill, not a channel (P6.1 row C confidence note states this explicitly). If warmth does not convert, the firm has **no channel at all** — neither content nor network — and the launch should pause at Stage 0 rather than proceed to Stage 1 on hope.

**Signal 5 — Which practice generates inbound.**
- **Validates the Section 1 recommendation:** inbound enquiries cluster on حدود القرار / زمن القرار (authority and latency), regardless of which content gets the most engagement.
- **Invalidates it:** engagement concentrates on the AI hooks while enquiries do not follow, or enquiries cluster on The Lab instead.
- **Consequence if invalidated:** the lead flagship changes — to مختبر القرار if enquiries cluster there. **Track enquiries and engagement as two separate series and never substitute one for the other**; the Phase 7 finding that credibility and AI leverage are perfectly inverse predicts exactly this divergence.

**Signal 6 — Price resistance and its shape.**
- **Validates the anchor:** objections are about scope, timing or sequencing.
- **Invalidates it:** objections are about the number itself, or buyers ask for a day rate.
- **Consequence if invalidated:** D12 is wrong — buyers *are* comparing to the independent tier, the $600–1,800/day benchmark **is** the binding ceiling, and Round 3's anchors are aggressive rather than conservative. Record the exact objection wording; "too expensive" and "what's your day rate" mean different things and imply different corrections.

**Signal 7 — Enactment, on the first delivery.**
- **Validates the T7 spine:** at least one authority threshold changed and is being exercised at 30 days post-close, and the client will say so by name.
- **Invalidates it:** the design was approved and nothing moved.
- **Consequence if invalidated:** the spine is a claim rather than a competence, and it must come out of the public messaging until it is evidenced. This is the most consequential signal in the set, and it arrives latest — it will not be available inside 90 days unless proof point 3 starts in Stage 0.

## 6.2 What would justify revisiting the Phase 8 architecture

Not any single invalidation. **The architecture is revisited if signals 2, 4 and 5 invalidate together** — i.e. buyers describe knowledge gaps, the warm network does not convert, and inbound does not cluster on the authority hooks. That combination says the practice architecture is pointed at a problem the accessible buyers do not have, which is a Phase 8 question, not a launch question.

A single invalidation moves a tactic. Signal 1 moves the channel. Signal 3 moves the entry-offer design. Signal 6 moves the price. **None of them individually reopens the territory, and treating them as though they do would produce exactly the churn this sequencing exists to prevent.**
