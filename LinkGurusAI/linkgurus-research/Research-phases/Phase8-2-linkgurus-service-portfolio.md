# ROUND 1 — Portfolio architecture · CLOSED

## The finding the architecture rests on

Across T1, T2, T3, T4 and T7 the artefact actually sold is the same thing every time: **roles, job architecture, decision rights, accountability.** What differs is not the work — it is the event that makes someone pay for it.

| Territory | The event that funds the same artefact |
|---|---|
| T1 | An organization being restructured |
| T2 | A large investment landed and the business did not change |
| T3 | An agent in production that nobody owns the output of |
| T4 | A founder who has become the bottleneck |
| T7 | A design that was approved and reverted |

This is not five businesses. It is **one competence sold into five rooms** — and it is the competence carrying the highest founder-credibility scores anywhere in the evidence base (FC 4 on P6.1 rows A, B and C; P1.1 Pool A classifies job architecture / spans and layers STRENGTHEN at High confidence, ~15 personally led projects).

T5 and T6 are different in kind. **T5 is a format, not a practice** — P8.1 states it directly: *"T5's method is a delivery mechanism inside T1, T4 and T7."* **T6 overlaps T5 rather than standing apart** — per D1 and the founder's own read: the same underlying method, differently articulated, with methodology tweaks, in the shape of the DSA AI Lab (Pilot Lab → Facilitator Training → Supervised Labs).

## The settled architecture

Three practices. Seven territories. One spine.

### Practice 1 — The Lab · T5 + T6
- **Buyer:** CEO · Chief Strategy Officer · Transformation head
- **Promise:** Where AI fits, what to fund, what to stop — decided in the room, with named owners and the conditions that would reverse it.
- **T5** — run the decision: problem framing, discovery, workflow sprints.
- **T6** — build their capability: their people run the cadence, on the founder's method and canvas.
- *Evidence spine:* P6.1 row D (46/70, AT 5, DP 4, PZ 4) · row G (46/70, the subtraction decision) · P2.2 row 13 (46/75) · Block D15 · Block E23 · P1.1 Pool D.

### Practice 2 — Restructuring & Org. Design · T1 + T4
- **Buyer:** CEO · owner · board
- **Promise:** Job architecture, roles and decision rights — for an organization being rebuilt, or one that has outgrown its founder.
- **T1** — restructure: job architecture, spans and layers. Private sector only, no regulatory framing (D2).
- **T4** — install: decision rights for a scale-up where everything routes through one person.
- *Evidence spine:* P6.1 row A (50/70, rank 1) · row C (46/70) · P3.1 segments 1 and 2 (composites 5400 and 4320, the two highest) · P2.2 row 2 (45/75).

### Practice 3 — The Operating Model · T2 + T3
- **Buyer:** COO · Transformation lead · CEO
- **Promise:** How the work actually runs once AI is inside it — and what still has to change when a large investment landed and the business did not.
- **T2 — Value on Hold (entry):** the investment landed and the way the business runs did not change. Business framing only (D3).
- **T3 — AI Operating Model (flagship):** where AI enters, which roles change, who owns what it decides.
- *Why the practice is named for what it builds rather than what it rescues:* T2's fatal flaw in P8.1 was *"you cannot build a pipeline against a problem nobody advertises having"* — stalled programmes are invisible and nobody says publicly that theirs failed. Buyers do say **"we need to design our operating model for AI"** out loud. Same work, advertised demand instead of hidden demand.
- *Evidence spine:* P6.1 row B (49/70, rank 2, EI 5) · row E (46/70) · P1.2 §1.1 ("the single most under-used item in the profile") · P2.2 row 5 (46/75, Emerging).
- *Why they are one practice and not two:* P8.1 states it — *"T2 is the credible on-ramp to T3, same collision, different technology."* One practice, two generations. A delivered T2 engagement is also the delivery evidence T3 requires, which today is zero.

### The spine — T7
Implementation accountability runs under all three practices rather than being sold as its own line.

**Why it is placed this way, not demoted.** T7 carries the highest pain score in the entire research base (P6.1 row F, PA 5) and the highest IP score (IP 4 — *"the most differentiated idea in the profile"*). It also carries buyer clarity 2, and P6.1 states the reason plainly: *burned buyers are not identifiable from outside; you cannot build a pipeline from a condition you cannot see.* Every T1, T2 and T4 buyer is one crisis away from being a T7 buyer. So T7 is the thing that makes the other three credible in the room, and a standalone recovery practice later once there is capacity to carry it.

**Positioning language for T7 is reserved for the founder** (D6). Two drafts were rejected: the recovery framing (reads as cleaning up after other firms) and the outcome-linked-fee framing (reads as materialistic). Both are excluded.

## Round 1 conclusion

All seven territories are placed. A buyer sees three practices, not seven services — which matters, because P5.2 records that Linkgurus today sits in the solo-generalist tier, and a seven-item service menu confirms that read rather than escaping it.

**Open concern, stated and carried:** seven territories, one founder, zero pipeline. The architecture's job is to make seven read as three. It does not solve capacity.

# ROUND 2 — Offer shaping · CLOSED

Structure per practice: an **entry** offer (low-risk first purchase), a **flagship** (the core paid engagement), and a **scale** offer (recurring, productized, or deliverable beyond the founder's own hours). Format, duration and price are Round 3.

## 2.1 — The Lab

### Entry — *Challenge Framing Workshop*

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it is** | A decision the client is already stuck on, framed and taken inside a single day. Not a diagnostic, not an assessment, not a scoping exercise for a bigger engagement. |
| **Input** | One real decision with money attached and no agreement in the room. |
| **The room** | The people who actually decide. Arabic. Half a day of preparation before it. |
| **Output** | A written decision record: the question as framed, the criteria, the options, the named owner, and the conditions that would reverse it. |
| **Boundary** | AI Problem Framing for one real challenge. Produces a framed challenge, decision record, evidence gaps, named authority, owner, and T7 condition. It does not include workflow redesign, prototyping, or capability transfer. |

**Why it is shaped this way.** The named objection in this category is *"a workshop that produces a poster, not a decision"* (P3.2 seg 3, "what creates risk"). The entry offer's entire job is to disprove that in one day at a price that makes yes easy. It also has the best-evidenced productization shape anywhere in the asset audit — fixed duration, defined deliverable, a named decision as output, and one instance of a client requesting exactly this unprompted (P1.1 Pool D; P1.2 §1.10; Block D15: four decision workshops with named outputs — scorecard sign-off, RACI agreement, two-scenario structural modelling, AI entry point in the target operating model).

**It is also the cheapest available test** of the largest open assumption in the research base (see flags below).

### Flagship — *The Decision Lab*

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it is** | Take one consequential challenge from a defensible frame through redesigned work and a tested concept—then make the Stop / Keep / Scale decision. |
| **Input** | One real, consequential challenge. |
| **The work** | Problem Framing — 1 day; Workflow Sprint — 2–4 days; Design Sprint — 2 days; Close — Stop / Keep / Scale decision plus T7 record. |
| **Output** | A defensible frame, redesigned work, a tested concept, and the Stop / Keep / Scale decision. |
| **Duration** | 5–7 working days. Do not reduce this to “one week”; the Workflow Sprint can extend the engagement to seven working days. |

**The design call that matters — do not sell subtraction on its own.** Deciding what to *kill* is the most differentiated element in this practice: P5.1 §3 finds the entire visible supply side oriented to adoption, nothing to subtraction. But P6.1 row G scores budget availability 2 for a structural reason — *the purchase releases money rather than consuming it, so it has no budget line of its own.*

Selling it **inside** the allocation decision does two things: it rides a funded door (GCC AI budgets — two-thirds of firms expect >30% of consulting spend on AI adoption, Source Global via P2.1), and it hands the engagement the only quotable number in the whole portfolio (recoverable budget). Evidence of the waste is strong: 42% of companies scrapped the majority of their AI initiatives in 2025, up from 17% in 2024; the average company abandoned 46% of proofs of concept (S&P Global via Fortune — FACT, global not MENA-specific).

### Scale — *Your Lab*

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it is** | A five-day bilingual employee capability program followed by two supervised Labs that transfer The Decision Lab into the client organization. |
| **Input** | A client who has run The Decision Lab once and wants it to become how they decide. |
| **The work** | Lab 1 is co-led by Linkgurus and the client. Lab 2 is client-led and observed by Linkgurus. Artifacts are calibrated after each Lab. |
| **Output** | Facilitator qualification only if both Labs meet the standard. If Lab 2 does not meet the standard, qualification is withheld and further supervision is separately scoped. |

**Source-evidence boundary.** DSA uses three supervised Labs. Linkgurus intentionally adapts that source mechanism into two supervised Labs; the DSA source description must not be rewritten as a two-Lab model.

> **Challenge Framing Workshop reveals and frames; The Decision Lab redesigns and tests; Your Lab transfers and qualifies.**

**The Decision Lab flagship template:** **Challenge-to-Decision Board — لوحة من التحدي إلى الحسم**.

**The trap.** Capability transfer normally destroys the market that created it. The clearest case in the evidence: DSA trained an internal facilitator pool at ELM in Riyadh, and that organization will never again buy external facilitation for routine work (P5.1 §6). *Anyone entering facilitation enters a market whose leaders monetize its disappearance.*

**The way through it — transfer the room, keep the cadence.** They run sessions; Linkgurus owns the quarterly re-run, the stop/keep review and the accountability trail. Training them then *increases* the volume of decisions flowing through the system instead of replacing the seller. This is the mechanism by which governance and audit advisory monetizes (analogy noted at P2.2 row 5 RR-4 — **[EXT]**, no MENA transaction evidences it).

**It also unlocks a funded door the flagship cannot reach.** P5.1 §6: in Saudi Arabia the co-funded budget line reads **تدريب**. Anything not packaged as training is invisible to it — and anything packaged as training competes with every certification course on price. The scale offer is the leg that can be packaged as training without dragging the flagship down to course pricing.

**Recurring-revenue caution.** P2.2 row 13 RR-2 stands unchanged: *no recurring-revenue form is evidenced anywhere in the facilitation or decision-design category.* This offer therefore has to be **designed**, not assumed to work by category norm.

### Flags carried openly on The Lab

1. **The load-bearing assumption.** That GCC buyers purchase decision work *standalone* rather than inside a consulting engagement they already bought. Unresolved since P2.1 Rec. 1; P5.2 W1 status is "cannot be determined." Challenge Framing Workshop is itself the cheapest test of it.
2. **What the canvas does and does not fix.** Finishing the method and canvas (D8) moves the **IP position** — it unblocks documentation, delegation, training and licensing, all of which were gated on Block C13. It does not move the **credibility position**: four decision workshops inside one project, no public facilitation record, no third-party validation of any kind (Block G29; P1.2 §1.11, FC 2). That is closed by delivering, naming clients and publishing.
3. **Method sourcing.** The Design Sprint method is open — published in the *Sprint* book and GV's free library, no licence required (P5.1 §5, FACT). DSA's canvases, curriculum and programme naming are proprietary packaging and their licence is UNKNOWN. Strategyzer canvases are CC BY-SA, whose share-alike clause blocks proprietary derivatives. An original instrument built on the open foundations is legally clean and is the only version with a moat; a translated adaptation is reproducible by any bilingual competitor in an afternoon (P1.1: DEPRECATE, High confidence).

### How The Lab lands in Arabic

Not names — Round 4 handles names. This is whether the *offer logic* survives the buying conversation in Arabic. Two of the three design calls change shape.

| Principle (English) | Arabic articulation | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| A decision, not a diagnostic | **«قرار… لا تقرير»** | **Stronger in Arabic than in English.** قرار and تقرير share the root ق-ر-ر — same root, opposite outcome. It lands directly on the complaint Arabic buyers actually voice: *we paid for a تقرير and nothing changed.* The English contrast ("diagnostic") carries almost nothing by comparison. |
| Don't sell "what to kill" alone | **«ما يُموَّل، وبأي ترتيب، وما يتوقف»** | **Needs a real change, not a translation.** "Kill" has no business register in Arabic, and the face problem is sharper in the Gulf — P6.1 row G notes nobody wants to be the person who commissioned the dead pilot. The fix is grammatical: **يتوقف** (*it stops*) not **نوقفه** (*we stop it*). Removing the agent removes the blame. |
| — same, framing | **«نُعيد توجيه الإنفاق إلى ما يُثمر»** | The subtraction sells as **money moving**, never as **something failing**. This matches the founder's own read of the Saudi buyer (Block B11): *"show them where the money will be spent and where it will return as a worthy investment."* |
| Transfer the room, keep the cadence | **«الجلسات يديرها فريقكم… والمراجعة الدورية معنا»** | "Cadence" does not survive — إيقاع reads musical. **المراجعة الدورية** is standard business Arabic and needs no explanation. The training leg says **تدريب** explicitly, because that is the word the co-funded budget line is written in (P5.1 §6). |

**Caveat, carried:** every construction above is built from Arabic **supply-side** vocabulary (Bakkah, balagh, RMG and similar), because P4.1 finding 6 states plainly that the scan found *essentially nothing that is unambiguously Arabic buyer language* in this space. That absence is a finding, not a search failure. These are proposals to test in a real room, not verified buyer wording.

## 2.2 — Restructuring & Org. Design

### The structural consequence of D2, stated once

Removing the regulatory framing removed **the only statutory clock in the entire evidence base.** T1 ranked 1st in Phase 6 at 50/70, and that rank came substantially from two scores the regulation supplied: urgency 5 (*"the only problem in this list of twenty with a statutory calendar the buyer does not control"*) and frequency 5 (annual cycles through 2028). Without it, this practice competes against two things that already exist and already have owners: *internal HR can absorb it*, and *just hire a strong COO* (P3.2 seg 1, seg 2).

This is not an argument to reinstate the regulatory framing — the founder's judgement on portfolio and access stands. It is a design constraint: **the practice must now manufacture its own trigger**, because it no longer inherits one. The entry offer is how.

### Entry — *Where Decisions Stop*

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it is** | Two or three decisions the leadership team keeps re-opening without closing, traced end to end — from the moment each is raised to the moment it is signed — and then costed downstream. |
| **Input** | The decisions the team is actually stuck on. |
| **The work** | One week. Interviews plus a trace of each decision's real path: who raised it, where it queued, who was actually holding it. |
| **Output** | A map of where decisions stop, who holds them, and the sequenced consequence of unblocking the top two. **Pointed at decisions, never at people.** |

**Why it is shaped this way.** This is the one diagnostic act in the profile with a concrete, evidenced instance: a client argued endlessly about a supporting function until the founder broke the jargon into a sequenced consequence chain, and the argument ended — *"they paused in awe after the realisation"* (Block C12; P1.2 §1.4 calls it a real diagnostic act with an identifiable tell). The entry offer is that act, productized.

**It speaks the customer's own register.** P4.1 §5 is explicit: the frustration language customers actually produce is *symptomatic and temporal* — "takes a week," "status updates," "waiting for authority" — while diagnostic vocabulary (accountability, decision rights, spans and layers) appears almost exclusively in supply-side text. *"Marketing that opens with the symptom clock speaks customer; marketing that opens with operating-model maturity speaks consultant."*

**The design guard.** P3.2 seg 2 names the risk precisely: *the diagnosis lands on the founder's own role.* An artefact that points at decisions rather than people is what makes it purchasable by the person who is also the problem.

### Flagship — *The Install*

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it is** | One engagement shape serving two situations — an organization being rebuilt (T1) and one that has outgrown its founder (T4). Same artefacts, different starting condition. |
| **Input** | A structure that was never designed, or one designed for a company that no longer exists. |
| **The work** | Job architecture, spans and layers, role definition, decision rights and authority thresholds — then the handover that makes them operate. |
| **Output** | A defined operating state reached: authority actually moved, roles actually held. Not a deck approved. |

**The design call that matters — the engagement cannot end at the design.** P3.2 seg 2's confirm/kill test for this exact segment is whether the structure was *enacted* (rights actually moved) or *presented* (deck to board, then drift). Ending at the design reproduces the failure the founder's own four-part thesis diagnoses (Block C14). **This is where T7 sits inside this practice**, and it is the reason the practice is more than an org-chart service.

**Scope guard.** Total rewards and salary-scale design are explicitly out of scope. Restructuring buyers will ask for them; the capability is founder-declared absent (Block B10, self-excluded; P1.1 classifies it DEPRECATE). Writing the exclusion into the scope is a credibility asset, not a gap.

**Pricing logic (carried to Round 3).** The competitor here is not a consultancy — it is hiring a strong COO or a fractional CHRO (P3.2 seg 2, "what blocks buying"). Price against the cost of a wrong senior hire, not against the $600–1,800/day independent benchmark that caps this tier (P2.1 §Q).

# ROUND 3 — Packaging & pricing · AWAITING REACTION

**Every number below is a proposal, not a decision.** No Linkgurus transaction at market price exists to calibrate any of them. Resistance to a specific anchor is useful signal and should be voiced.

## The pricing problem, stated plainly

Three facts constrain everything here, and they point in opposite directions.

1. **The only two price data points in existence are minimum-charge engagements** — one KSA, one Egypt, three months each, both of which bought the founder rather than the firm (Block A1 — FACT). They establish no price point at all except a floor that is too low.
2. **The stated rate of USD 450/hour has never been realized in a transaction** and is directly contradicted by those two engagements (Block E22; P1.2 §0.1 — "a price never tested in a market is a target, not a rate").
3. **The founder's own diagnosis is that the historical error was under-charging** (Block E21: *"I was under charging my services… my energy and time are most expensive to me"*). So the correction has to be upward, but from a base of zero market evidence.

Two market ceilings press down on any number chosen: GCC independent-consultant day rates run **$600–1,800/day** (P2.1 §Q) — and P5.2 records that this is *the tier Linkgurus currently competes in.* AI-native boutiques market at **40–60% below Big 4** and are explicitly compressing the boutique price ceiling (P2.1 §M).

## Three pricing principles applied throughout

**1. Price the product, not the day.** Every offer below is priced against the value at stake or against the buyer's real alternative — never as founder-days × a rate. The moment an offer is quoted in days, it re-enters the $600–1,800/day tier the whole repositioning exists to escape (P5.2).

**2. Entry offers are priced to remove the decision, not to earn.** They must sit below the threshold at which a GCC private-sector CEO or COO needs anyone else's sign-off. Their return is the flagship, not the fee.

**3. Each practice's real competitor sets its anchor** — and it is different in each case:

| Practice | The buyer's actual alternative | What that anchors against |
|---|---|---|
| The Lab | A Big 4 AI assessment, a vendor pilot, an offsite, or a €35–40k English cohort | Workshop-to-mid-consulting band |
| Restructuring & Org. Design | **Hiring a strong COO or fractional CHRO** (P3.2 seg 2) | The cost of a wrong senior hire — not a day rate |
| The Operating Model | A boutique AI engagement at $75k–$500k, or doing nothing while the benefits case erodes | The bottom of the boutique AI band |

# ROUND 4 — Arabic offer naming · AWAITING SHORTLIST

Names for the **offers**, not for the company. Corporate naming is Phase 9.

## Rules applied

- **Benefit-oriented and memorable, never a category label.** «استشارات استراتيجية» describes a category; it is not a name anyone remembers or searches for.
- **"Arabic" appears in no name** (D4).
- **Built on vocabulary confirmed live in Arabic business content** (P4.1 §1, §5, Arabic finding 3): غموض المسؤوليات · عدم وضوح السلطات · تأخر القرارات · فرط المعلومات · الخوف من الأخطاء.

- **إعادة الهيكلة excluded** — it carries redundancies and produces «حالة من القلق الشديد» (P4.1 §4). بنية is used as the clean substitute for structure.
- **No transliteration of English method names.** «تصميم سبرنت» effectively does not exist in Arabic business discourse (P4.1, FACT within scan reach) — the concept has no Arabic handle, which is opportunity, not a licence to borrow.
- **Short.** One to three words. Sayable in a corridor, findable in a search.

## A naming system, if wanted

A pattern emerged without being forced, and it is worth seeing before shortlisting because it makes nine offers read as one firm:

| Tier | Natural Arabic form | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Entry** | **A question or a named problem** — they exist to make an invisible problem visible | عُقدة القرار · القيمة المعلّقة · أين تتوقف القرارات |
| **Flagship** | **A structure or a decision** — what gets built | مَن يقرر · ما يستمر · مسؤولٌ بالاسم |
| **Scale** | **Something periodic** — الدوري recurs naturally in all three | دورة القرار · الفحص الدوري · الجرد الدوري |

The third row is the useful one: **الدوري appears organically in all three scale offers**, which reinforces the portfolio-wide finding that Linkgurus keeps the review cycle. That is a system the buyer can feel without being told.

**A second cross-portfolio tie:** the agentless **يتوقف** appears in both The Lab's flagship logic («ما يتوقف») and Structure's entry («أين تتوقف القرارات»). Using it deliberately in both makes the face-preserving grammar a house style rather than a one-off.

## Standing caveat on all of the above

Every name is built from Arabic **supply-side** vocabulary. P4.1 finding 6 states plainly that the scan found *essentially nothing that is unambiguously Arabic buyer language* in this space — an absence that is a finding, not a search failure. These are strong candidates to say aloud in a real room and watch land; they are not verified buyer wording.

**The operating logic:** questions and symptoms attract; deliverables get bought. Every hook is a question or a symptom (زمن القرار · مَن يقرر · من يملك النتائج النهائية · العائد المفقود); every name is a deliverable or a room (خارطة الصلاحيات · إرساء النموذج المؤسسي · سجل المسؤولية · مجلس القرار).

### Three consequences of the founder's selection

**1. A brand-voice signal, recorded for Phase 9.** Every elliptical or clever candidate was rejected — ما يستمر، دورة القرار، عُقدة القرار، مسؤولٌ بالاسم، انحراف البنية. Every clear, senior-register candidate was kept. **Linkgurus's Arabic voice is precise and senior, not playful.** This is founder-selected evidence, not an analyst's inference, and it should constrain Phase 9 naming.

**2. Practice 2 produced the strongest family in the portfolio, and the founder built it.**

> **خارطة الصلاحيات → إرساء النموذج المؤسسي → فحص الصلاحيات الدوري**
> *Map the authority → delegate it → inspect it.*

Three offers, one root word, a visible ladder a buyer can follow without explanation. No other practice is this coherent, and the coherence emerged from the founder's picks rather than the proposed set.

**3. القيمة المعلّقة must carry the name, not العائد المفقود.** *Pending* value is recoverable, and recovery is what is being sold. *Lost* value is written off, and nobody buys back something already declared gone. العائد المفقود is retained as the sharper line for buyers who have already reached alarm.

### Two notes carried forward

- **حوكمة وكلاء الذكاء الاصطناعي is a component, not an alternative name.** Operating-model design and agent governance are two parts of the same flagship. It is treated as a named module inside نموذج تشغيل وكلاء الذكاء الاصطناعي **and** as the term to claim in content — P4.1 Arabic finding 5 confirms this vocabulary is forming in 2026 and P8.1 records that in Arabic "nothing exists at all." Claiming it early is a defensibility route that requires no delivery evidence. *(Standard orthography: الاصطناعي, hamzat wasl, no hamza.)*
- **The الدوري system is now partial.** It survives in Practice 2 (فحص الصلاحيات الدوري) but دورة القرار and الجرد الدوري were not selected. Recurrence therefore lives in each scale offer's **descriptor line** rather than its name — مختبركم and سجل المسؤولية are recurring by construction regardless.

## The structural insight: two acquisition engines, not one

The Phase 7 finding — founder credibility and AI leverage are perfectly inverse — reappears here as a **go-to-market** problem: the content asset points at AI, and the credibility asset points at structure.

The resolution is not to choose. It is to run two engines that do not compete for the same effort:

| Engine | Sells | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Content** → The Lab | مجلس القرار, then مختبر القرار | Two years of AI content already points here. The audience exists. The purchase is small and fast. |
| **Network** → Restructuring & Org. Design | خارطة الصلاحيات, then إرساء النموذج المؤسسي | The five contacts and the alumni base are far more likely to sit on a structure problem than on a stuck AI decision. This sale happens in a conversation, not a funnel. |
| **Position only** → The Operating Model | نموذج تشغيل وكلاء الذكاء الاصطناعي | Claimed publicly from day one; sold from Q2 once T2-shaped evidence exists. |

## The 90 days

### Days 1–14 — Open the doors that are already there

Nothing here costs money and all of it has been open since Phase 1.

1. **Promote to the 2,500 list — an offer, not a course.** This resolves the single cheapest and longest-open question in the entire engagement: does the audience contain budget holders or individual practitioners? P1.2 §3.4 states the disproof condition plainly — if it is practitioners, this is a course-buying audience and not a consulting channel, and the two convert at different orders of magnitude.
2. **Approach all five GCC contacts.** Not a pitch — a specific ask, and five conversations designed to *falsify*. P3.2 flags that five of seven real-job hypotheses already converged on the analyst's own prior; these conversations must be built to disconfirm, not to confirm. **They also double as the 8–10 buyer interviews open since P2.1 Rec. 1** — the discriminating question being whether buyers describe a *knowledge* gap or an *alignment* gap.
3. **Enumerate the Geidea/ARASCO alumni base.** It has never been listed. P1.1 rates it potentially the highest-quality warm route into KSA.

### Days 15–45 — Build the content engine on the hooks already chosen

The four hooks confirmed in Round 4 **are** the content calendar. They were selected as attention-getters; this is what they are for.

| Hook | The content it carries |
|---|---|
| **زمن القرار** | The symptom clock — *a decision that needs a day takes a week.* P4.1 §5: this temporal register is the customers' own language and the safest possible foundation. |
| **حدود القرار** | Authority and delegation — *تفويض العمل دون إرساء النموذج المؤسسي.* |
| **العائد المفقود** | Value paid for and not received. CFO-legible. |
| **حوكمة وكلاء الذكاء الاصطناعي** | The Arabic accountability vocabulary for agents that **nobody has written yet** (P4.1 Arabic finding 5; P8.1 T3). |

**The highest-leverage single act available in this window:** publish the enterprise-system transformation story. P1.1 classifies it REPACKAGE and calls it *"the strongest available credibility bridge into the AI direction — stronger than the two years of AI content,"* and records that it is **currently invisible in all public material.** It costs nothing to publish and it is the only thing that makes نموذج تشغيل وكلاء الذكاء الاصطناعي credible before a single engagement is delivered.

**Discipline for all of it:** open with the symptom, never the framework. P4.1 §5 — *"marketing that opens with the symptom clock speaks customer; marketing that opens with operating-model maturity speaks consultant."*

### Days 46–90 — First sale

**First offer sold: مجلس القرار** (Challenge Framing Workshop, $6,000–8,000).

Why this one, on the evidence:

- **Lowest friction in the portfolio.** One day, one decision, one artefact, a price inside discretionary spend.
- **The content engine already points at it.** Two years of AI content and the existing audience sit on top of this problem, not on top of job architecture.
- **It is the only offer with four delivered precedents.** Block D15 — four decision workshops with named outputs, plus one instance of a client requesting the format unprompted (P1.2 §1.10).
- **It is the cheapest test of the largest open assumption in the research base** — whether GCC buyers purchase decision work standalone rather than inside an engagement they already bought (P2.2 row 13 MD-3; P5.2 W1, status "cannot be determined").
- **It produces a case study fastest.** One day of delivery to a nameable outcome — and with discounting off the table (D11), speed to first case study is the only lever left.

**Realistic first buyer:** a CEO or strategy head inside the existing audience or the five-contact network, sitting on an AI or investment decision that has been re-opened more than twice. Not a cold enterprise buyer — P5.2 and Block A1 are unambiguous that both lifetime clients bought the founder, not the firm, and that is the mechanism that will produce the third one too.

**Running in parallel, through the network rather than the funnel:** خارطة الصلاحيات (one week, $9,000–12,000) to whichever of the five contacts describes a structure problem rather than a decision problem. This does not compete with the content engine for effort, and it is the practice where founder credibility is strongest.
