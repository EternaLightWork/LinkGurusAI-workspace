# Situation-Based Customer Segmentation — Linkgurus
## Phase 2.3 companion to the Macro Market Scan and Category Landscape Map. Prepared 13 August 2026.

**What this is.** Seventeen potential customer segments defined by *situation* — strategic condition, trigger event, problem structure, decision dynamics — not by size or industry. It does **not** recommend a segment. Segments unflattering to the facilitated-decision-design thesis and to Linkgurus's current business are included deliberately (rows ranked 14, 15, 17, and the caveats on rank 12).

**Evidence base.** Phase 2.1 Market Scan; Category Landscape Map (Phase 2.2); Unfair Advantage Map; Strategic Asset Audit v2; founder Block A–G responses.

**Scoring.** Seven factors, each 1–5: Pain (P), Urgency (U), Budget (B), Accessibility (A), Founder Advantage (FA), Differentiation (D), Scalability (S). **Composite = P×U×B×A×FA×D×S** (max 78,125). The multiplicative form is deliberate: a single 1 collapses the composite, which correctly models a binding constraint (no budget, no access) that averaging would hide. Founder Advantage is scored by the external-evidence standard (certifications, delivery record, third-party validation), so it is depressed across all rows; scores are relative. Assumptions are flagged **[A: …]** in the rationale section.

## Reading notes

- **The composite is a screening device, not a verdict.** The multiplicative form makes low single scores decisive: the two highest-budget segments in the table (15, 16) rank near the bottom because access is a genuine binding constraint, and the highest-pain segment (7) is dragged down by targetability. That is the correct behavior of the model, but a partnership, a subcontract, or one warm introduction changes an A-score by a point and re-orders the table substantially. Sensitivity to single assumptions is high.
- **Thesis balance check (per stop conditions).** Decision-design-relevant segments land at ranks 3, 10, 12, 14, and 17 — one strong, four middling-to-weak, including the two segments (14, 17) where the confirmed Arabic facilitation supply and the build-in-house pattern cut directly against the thesis. OD/talent-strength segments land at 1, 2, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 13, 16. Learning/productized segments at 4 and 11. Neither thesis was protected.
- **Language as a segmentation variable** appears twice: as its own cross-cutting segment (12) and embedded in the FA/D scores of segments 1, 3, 8. Its value is capped by an unresolved data point: the founder's ambiguous answer on boardroom-standard delivery in both languages (Block G28). A one-line clarification changes four rows.
- **What would re-rank this table fastest:** (1) the 8–10 buyer interviews from Phase 2.1 Rec. 1 — they test the load-bearing assumption under rank 3; (2) activating the five named contacts — it moves A-scores in segments 1, 2, 8; (3) one promotion test on the 2,500-person list — it prices segment 4's B-score with data instead of assumption.
- No single segment is recommended, per stop conditions.
