# Positioning Map — Linkgurus Repositioning
## Phase 5.2 companion to Phase 5.1 (Competitive Landscape). Prepared 13 August 2026.

**What this is.** A text-form positioning map of the Phase 5.1 landscape, built on the dimensions that actually separate strategic alternatives in the evidence — not on claimed qualities. It places every Phase 5.1 competitor plus Linkgurus as positioned today (Phase 1 findings), identifies competition-density zones, and classifies each whitespace. Per stop conditions, it does not recommend which whitespace to pursue.

---

## Axis selection — chosen, and rejected, with reasons

### Rejected dimensions (they do not separate)

- **Strategic ↔ Operational:** every player from McKinsey to aiworkshop.ae claims the strategic end; actual position would have to be inferred from ticket size, which is captured better by the buyer-tier descriptor below.
- **Advice ↔ Execution / Diagnosis ↔ Implementation as a *claim*:** Phase 5.1's first pattern finding is that "from strategy to execution" is now claimed by every tier — a dimension on which everyone claims the same end separates nothing. The *economic reality* behind it (what the buyer actually receives) does separate, and is used as Axis 2.
- **Human-centric ↔ Technology-centric:** "human + AI" is table stakes across every tier (Phase 5.1, pattern 1). As rhetoric it separates nothing; as substance it survives only as the problem-locus descriptor (org-side vs tech-side work).
- **Traditional ↔ AI-native:** separates exactly one archetype (AI-native boutiques) from everyone else; too binary to organize a map, and converging fast as incumbents embed AI (McKinsey Lilli, BCG 25% AI revenue — P2.1).

### Axis 1 (horizontal): **English-first global method ↔ Arabic-native local delivery**

Chosen because it is the single most factual, least imitated separator in the evidence. Every under-served zone found across three independent scans (P2.1 §N, Phase 4 VoC, Phase 5.1 §4) sits at the Arabic end; DSA's entire Gulf record is English fly-in; the Arabic-native side is populated almost exclusively by training houses and content-neutral facilitation. Position on this axis is verifiable from delivery evidence (site language, materials, staffing), not marketing.

### Axis 2 (vertical): **Done-for-you delivery ↔ Capability/asset transfer**

What does the buyer actually take home: an outcome delivered by the vendor (bespoke advisory, embedded implementation, facilitated rooms run by the vendor) — or a capability/asset that reduces future dependence on the vendor (training, certification, method IP, software, templates)? Chosen because it subsumes Customization ↔ Productization and Project-based ↔ Continuous into the economically decisive split, and because Phase 5.1's deepest systems finding lives on it: the market's dominant sale (capability transfer — DSA train-the-trainer, DesignThinkers academy, Bakkah certification funnel, DFF in-housing, DIY tools) structurally shrinks the done-for-you market. A firm's position here determines whom it is actually competing with, regardless of category label.

### Two descriptors carried in words (not axes)

- **Problem locus:** organization/people-side ↔ technology-side ↔ decision/innovation-process-side. Kept as a descriptor because it is trimodal, not bipolar.
- **Buyer tier:** government/enterprise ↔ mid-market/SME ↔ individual. Kept as a descriptor because in this landscape it is nearly constant (almost everyone targets enterprise/government — itself a finding).

## Competition-density zones

### Zone A — English-first × done-for-you (org/tech advisory & implementation): **HIGH competition**
MBB, Big 4, Accenture, Korn Ferry, Mercer, YCP Renoir, AI-native boutiques, plus the upper independent tier. Credential-gated, brand-gated, and being repriced from two directions at once (outcome-linked incumbent fees; 40–60% boutique discounting). This is where linkgurus.net currently *claims* to sit.

### Zone D — Arabic-native × capability transfer (the training shelf): **HIGH competition**
Bakkah, RMG, TAM's program layer, government academies, DesignThinkers' academy arm, plus every certification reseller from P2.1. HRDF funding makes it liquid but price-anchored at course rates. The personal brand currently sits here.

### Zone B — English-first × capability transfer (methods, software, certification): **MODERATE competition**
DSA, Orgvue, aiworkshop.ae, DIY floor. Fewer players, but structurally self-limiting economics (each sale reduces future demand; open methodology caps price from below — P5.1 systems findings). DSA's repeat Gulf revenue shows the zone transacts; its train-the-trainer pattern shows what buyers actually buy.

### Zone C — Arabic-native × done-for-you (facilitation and programs): **MODERATE competition, thin at advisory depth**
ES Facilitation (content-neutral rooms), TAM (national-scale programs), DesignThinkers' facilitation arm, Arabic-capable independents. Populated but shallow: nobody in it carries a named decision method or org-design depth of their own — the room-running capacity exists; the content IP does not.

### LOW-competition areas (occupied but sparse)
- **Arabic-native done-for-you org-design advisory at boutique tier** — Bakkah's consulting arm and some independents; sparse relative to the KSA regulatory demand driver (Nitaqat Mutawar — P2.1 FACT).
- **English capability-transfer for *decision* methods specifically** — effectively DSA alone at enterprise grade (aiworkshop.ae at solo scale).

## Whitespace outline — each area, and what it actually is

**W1. Arabic-native, method-led decision facilitation (decision named as the product, proprietary method brought to the room)**
- *Evidence:* No occupant found across three independent search efforts (P2.1 §N, Phase 4, P5.1 disclosure). Nearest neighbors: ES Facilitation (Arabic rooms, no method), RMG (Arabic decision *courses*), DSA (method, English, fly-in).
- *Status:* **Cannot be determined from current evidence — explicitly not classified as a real opportunity.** The only demand proof anywhere near it is DSA's Gulf revenue, which is (a) English, (b) mostly capability transfer, (c) innovation/product buyers rather than OD buyers. Whether Arabic-preferring executives would buy the *decision* as a standalone product is the unvalidated load-bearing assumption flagged in Phase 2.2 (row 13, MD-3), and the Arabic-demand premise itself rests on the unresolved G28 question (boardroom language). The Phase 2.1 buyer interviews remain the only instrument that can move this from UNKNOWN. The mirage warning from Phase 2.2 applies: unentered may mean unevaluated — or evaluated and declined (DSA serves the Gulf without localizing; that is weak evidence localization isn't worth it to the incumbent).

**W2. Business-side AI workflow / role redesign delivered in Arabic (org design × AI adoption, advisory not training)**
- *Evidence:* Under-served per three scans; demand drivers are real and dated (Nitaqat Mutawar April 2026 — FACT; Deloitte's 84%-haven't-redesigned-jobs; agent production deployments). Arabic supply stops at the course tier (RMG, Bakkah); English supply exists at every tier.
- *Status:* **Emerging category — with one unproven premise.** The *problem* is funded and datable (the strongest demand evidence of any whitespace here); what is unproven is that buyers require it *in Arabic* rather than accepting the English-delivered incumbents they already use. Saudi CEOs' public posture is AI confidence, not paralysis (PwC 29th, Phase 4 §3), and bilingual buyers may not pay a language premium. Emerging ≠ open: Deloitte owns the narrative, and DesignThinkers/aiworkshop.ae could each move here within a quarter (P5.1 disclosure).

**W3. Post-decision implementation accountability at boutique ticket size (between "workshop ends" and "six months embedded")**
- *Evidence:* The implementation gap is the most repeated buyer complaint in the VoC scan (consultant cynicism; ERP spreadsheet reversion); YCP Renoir proves demand at program scale; nothing found between workshop-scale and program-scale tickets.
- *Status:* **Real problem, unproven economics — possibly an unattractive niche.** No recurring-revenue form is evidenced anywhere in the category (Phase 2.2 row 13, RR-2); the ticket size in between may be structurally unprofitable (too big for a workshop budget, too small to justify sales cost), which would explain the vacancy better than oversight does. Vacancy with a plausible economic explanation should be presumed unattractive until a transaction disproves it.

**W4. SME / mid-market org design (either language)**
- *Evidence:* Every substantial P5.1 row targets enterprise/government; the mid-market is left to solo consultants and templates. But: Egyptian mid-market is cost-extractive (founder's B11 read, small sample); GCC mid-market day-rates are capped by the independent tier ($600–1,800/day).
- *Status:* **Mostly an unattractive niche, with one dated exception left open.** The exception: KSA mid-market employers under Nitaqat Mutawar (Phase 2.3's top-ranked segment) have regulatory urgency that normal SME discretion lacks. Whether that urgency converts to advisory budgets rather than compliance-paperwork budgets (the existing Saudization-consultancy industry's territory — tawteen.sa, ihr.sa et al.) is undetermined.

**W5. Formal Decision Quality (SDG/Stanford lineage) in Arabic**
- *Evidence:* Confirmed-absent supply (P2.1: no SDG Arabic materials, no SDP MENA chapter, no Decision Frameworks MENA office); zero demand signals at the DQ tier; Arabic demand evidenced only at the generic-course tier.
- *Status:* **Demand not proven — the map's clearest mirage candidate.** Empty space with zero demand evidence and a disqualifying credential gap for this founder (Phase 2.2 row 14, FC-1). Carried on the map for completeness, not promise.

**W6. The corridor between Zone B and Zone C (bilingual method + local done-for-you delivery combined)**
- *Evidence:* DesignThinkers is the only player straddling it, and its method is design thinking (innovation lens), not org design or decision design. DSA has the method without the geography/language; ES Facilitation has the geography/language without the method.
- *Status:* **Structurally real as a gap; commercially undetermined.** Note it exists only as long as its neighbors don't move: it is one partnership away from closing (e.g., any Arabic facilitation house licensing or building a decision method — openness of the underlying methods cuts both ways, P5.1 IP finding).

## Reading note on Linkgurus's two points

The entity and the personal brand occupy different zones, different axes-ends, and different buyer tiers — the entity claims the most contested zone on the map (A) with no admissible evidence, while the brand transacts in a crowded zone (D) at the lowest ticket altitude. No other player on this map is split this way; every competitor's public position and delivery evidence point at the same zone. This is the spatial restatement of the Phase 1 COMBINE finding, recorded here as a map fact, not a recommendation.

## Stop-condition compliance
No whitespace is recommended. W1's status is deliberately left as "cannot be determined" rather than resolved optimistically; the instruments that could resolve it (buyer interviews, G28 clarification, list-promotion test) are already logged as open items from prior phases. Whitespace selection belongs to Phases 7–8 once all evidence streams combine.
