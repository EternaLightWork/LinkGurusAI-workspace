## Design Sprint Academy — mandated deep-dive findings

1. **Delivery model (FACT, from site):** two modes — (a) expert facilitation, DSA runs the session; (b) corporate training / train-the-trainer, building client-internal facilitator pools. The Gulf engagements followed mode (b)-heavy patterns: ELM was a two-year capability-building relationship explicitly aimed at internal facilitators; ENOC was senior-leader training on applying sprints to their own challenges.
2. **Pricing signals (FACT where public):** AI Facilitator Training €2,500 pp for 3-day Berlin cohorts (Sept/Oct 2026, from site); historic bootcamps ~$4,500–5,000 pp (secondary source). Enterprise facilitation/engagement pricing unpublished. INFERENCE: per-participant training economics put a 15-person corporate cohort at roughly €35–40k — workshop-scale, an order of magnitude below consulting programs, consistent with Phase 2.2's BA-3 scoring of the category.
3. **Target buyer (FACT):** enterprise product/strategy/innovation teams making "high-stakes" AI/strategy/product decisions — heads of innovation, product leadership, increasingly AI program owners. Not CHROs, not org-design buyers.
4. **MENA/Arabic presence beyond ENOC (FACT, bounded):** ELM Company Riyadh (Oct 2023 engagement; invited back 2024; "two years of building innovation capability in Saudi Arabia" is their own blog title), a Jahez-sponsored Riyadh workshop (Dec 2023), ALEC Holdings (~2026, 55 participants → 6 validated AI use cases, self-reported), DIFC Academy sessions (Dubai), and ENOC Dubai. **No MENA office, no Arabic-language materials or delivery found anywhere** — site, blog, and search all English-only. All Gulf work is fly-in.
5. **IP status of the methodology (the partner-or-competitor question):**
   - **Design Sprint: open.** Created by Jake Knapp at Google/GV (2010), published in full in the 2016 *Sprint* book and GV's free online library; taught commercially by many unaffiliated firms (AJ&Smart, design-sprint.com, Rhinocorp, CoHive…). No license is required to run or teach design sprints. FACT.
   - **Problem Framing / AI Problem Framing: open method, proprietary packaging.** Problem-framing techniques are public-domain management practice; what DSA owns is its *branded curriculum, canvases, certification, and trademark-style naming* ("AI Problem Framing," "AI Workflow Sprint" as DSA program names). INFERENCE (trademark registrations not verified): copying DSA's materials would infringe copyright; building an original Arabic-language decision-facilitation method on the open foundations (Sprint book, GV library, public problem-framing literature, plus CC BY-SA Strategyzer tools with share-alike obligations) would not. Phase 2.2's warning stands in the other direction too: what is legally open to Linkgurus is equally open to every bilingual competitor — openness cuts both ways and confers no moat.
   - **Consequence for the partner-vs-competitor question (stated, not recommended):** nothing in the IP position forces a choice; DSA licenses nothing that an Arabic equivalent would need, and DSA's own train-the-trainer model shows it monetizes capability transfer rather than method exclusivity.

## Pattern Findings (outline)

### 1. What almost everyone says
- **"Human + AI"** — Korn Ferry's campaign, Mercer's "human-centric," Deloitte's "humans with agents," IDC's "instruments, not co-workers." The phrase is now table stakes across every tier.
- **"From strategy to execution" / "not just decks"** — MBB (outcome-linked fees), YCP Renoir (implementation heritage), AI-native boutiques (build + advise), AstroLabs ("beyond just workshops"), DSA ("decisions not fake alignment"). Every layer of the market now claims the execution gap as its differentiator, which means none of them differentiates by claiming it.
- **"Capability building, not dependency"** — DSA train-the-trainer, DesignThinkers academy, DFF in-housing, TAM programs, Bakkah's certification funnel. The market has converged on selling the fishing rod.
- **"Practical / hands-on / real workflows"** — aiworkshop.ae, RMG simulations, AstroLabs labs, YCP Renoir embedding.

### 2. Claims that have become generic
"AI readiness." "Future of work." "Transformation partner." "Measurable ROI/outcomes." "Human-centered." "Vision-2030-aligned" (in KSA, this now appears on essentially every provider's site regardless of substance). "Better decisions" is itself heading this way — DSA's tagline, aiworkshop.ae's "Verify & Decide," Bakkah's "help organizations reach the best decisions."

### 3. Over-served problems
- **AI awareness/fundamentals training** — RMG, Bakkah, aiworkshop.ae, HEMOdata masterclasses, government academies, plus every LMS. Saturated in both languages.
- **AI roadmaps/readiness assessments** — Big 4, integrators, AI-native boutiques, SDAIA-Oliver Wyman national framework (P2.1: overcrowded, high confidence).
- **Generic design-thinking/innovation workshops** — global networks, local hubs, open templates, in-house cohorts.
- **Talent-trend thought leadership** — Korn Ferry, Mercer, Deloitte produce near-interchangeable survey reports.

### 4. Under-served problems (evidence-bounded, not asserted)
- **Business-side AI workflow/role redesign in Arabic** — the Arabic tier stops at courses (RMG, Bakkah); the facilitated/advisory tier is English (DSA, aiworkshop.ae default). Consistent with P2.1's "nascent, mostly English" verdict — supported, not newly proven.
- **Decision-process facilitation sold standalone** — only DSA and (marginally) aiworkshop.ae name the *decision* as the product; everyone else buries it inside programs, training, or platforms. Whether buyers will purchase it standalone remains the unvalidated load-bearing assumption (Phase 2.2, row 13, MD-3).
- **Post-decision implementation accountability at boutique scale** — YCP Renoir serves it embedded at program scale; below that ticket size, nothing found between "consultant leaves after the workshop" and "consultant moves in for six months."
- **SME/mid-market org design** — every substantial row above targets enterprise/government; the mid-market is left to solo consultants and templates.

### 5. Language becoming commoditized
"Transformation," "innovation," "empower," "AI-powered," "workshop," "sprint" (openly published, sold at every price from a $30 book to €2,500 certifications), "facilitation" (IAF-certifiable, supplied in Arabic already — ES Facilitation), and in Arabic: التحول الرقمي، التمكين، الابتكار، ورش العمل are as worn as their English counterparts (every Saudi provider site uses all four).

### 6. Systems-thinking collisions — unrelated-looking competitors fighting for the same budget
- **Hackathon programs vs. advisory (TAM/AstroLabs vs. consultancies):** a Saudi entity that needs to *decide what to do with AI* can buy a TAM hackathon from the program/events budget, an Accenture pilot from the technology budget, a Deloitte assessment from the transformation budget, or a facilitated decision workshop from the L&D budget. Four procurement doors, one underlying need — the facilitation-shaped purchase is competing with three better-established doors.
- **Software+alliance vs. boutique advisory (Orgvue+PwC):** each Orgvue license converts future org-design advisory spend into software subscription + Big-4 integration — the advisory pool boutiques fish in is being drained upstream.
- **Train-the-trainer vs. facilitation-as-a-service (DSA vs. DSA, and everyone):** the market's dominant sale (capability transfer) structurally destroys the repeat-facilitation market; DSA's ELM engagement created a Saudi internal facilitator pool that will never again buy external facilitation for routine sprints. Anyone entering facilitation enters a market whose leaders are monetizing its disappearance.
- **Training houses vs. facilitators (Bakkah/RMG vs. ES Facilitation/DSA):** the Arabic buyer's HRDF-fundable budget line reads "تدريب" (training); a decision workshop that isn't packaged as training is invisible to that budget, while one that is competes with every certification course on price.
- **Surveys vs. engagements (Korn Ferry/Mercer vs. MBB):** the CHRO-as-transformation-leader shift (Consultancy-ME) puts HR consultancies and strategy firms in the same bid for the same mandate — compressing the space between them where independent OD advisory used to sit.

## Stop-condition compliance — the MENA/Arabic search disclosure

**What was searched for (this phase, beyond P2.1's confirmed set):** Saudi/UAE providers of hackathon-style AI adoption programs and innovation programs (found: TAM, AstroLabs — both real, both adjacent, neither offering decision-design as such); Arabic-language facilitation-for-decisions services («ميسر/تيسير» + «ورش عمل استراتيجية» + «اتخاذ القرار») (found: ES Facilitation, whose own Arabic copy promises "the environment necessary for decision-making" — the closest Arabic-language claim to the thesis found anywhere); "innovation sprint / strategy sprint / decision workshop" providers in Riyadh/Jeddah/Abu Dhabi (found: keynote-trainer tier and global remote providers, nothing MENA-headquartered with a named decision method); Arabic executive AI-decision programs (found: RMG's "AI for Leaders and Decision-Makers" — course tier).

**What this does and does not establish:** The MENA/Arabic market is **not empty** — it contains Arabic-native facilitation capacity (ES Facilitation, IAF-linked), bilingual design-thinking delivery (DesignThinkers), Arabic AI-decision *training* (RMG), innovation-program delivery at national scale (TAM), and English-language decision-method imports with repeat Gulf clients (DSA). What was **not found** — after this phase's searches plus Phase 2.1's dedicated scan and the Phase 4 VoC scan — is a MENA-headquartered or Arabic-language provider that names *the decision itself* as the product and brings a proprietary decision method to it. Per the Phase 4 evidentiary standard: this is absence-of-findings across three independent search efforts with different tooling, which is stronger than a single scan but still not proof of absence — LinkedIn, closed procurement, and word-of-mouth markets remain invisible, and adjacent suppliers (ES Facilitation, DesignThinkers, aiworkshop.ae) could each reposition into that claim within a quarter.

No positioning against any specific competitor is recommended in this document, per stop conditions.
