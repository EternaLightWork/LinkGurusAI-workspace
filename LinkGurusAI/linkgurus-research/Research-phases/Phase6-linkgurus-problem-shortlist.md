# Ranked Problem Shortlist — Linkgurus
## Phase 6.1. The 20 most commercially relevant problems, scored across 14 dimensions. Prepared 13 August 2026.

**What this is.** Twenty specific problems, drawn from all four research streams (internal capability, customer segments, competitor gaps, market trends), each scored 1–5 on the fourteen mandated dimensions and ranked by total. It does **not** choose or recommend a problem, and it does not propose a positioning. Per stop conditions, no score was raised because a problem sounds novel or lowered because it sounds ordinary.

**Evidence base.** P0 Strategic Research Brief · P1.1 Strategic Asset Audit v2 · P1.2 Founder Unfair Advantage Map · P2.1 MENA Market Scan · P2.2 Category Landscape Map · P3.1 Situation Segments · P3.2 JTBD Analysis · P4.1 VoC Language Scan · P5.1 Competitive Landscape · P5.2 Positioning Map · Questions_answered.md (founder Blocks A–G, the primary source under Phase 1).

## Scoring conventions — read before the table

**Orientation.** All fourteen dimensions are oriented so **5 = more favorable for Linkgurus**. Two are therefore inverted from their natural reading:

- **EA — Existing alternatives:** 5 = alternatives are absent or structurally inadequate for the job; 1 = the buyer is already adequately served.
- **CI — Competitive intensity:** 5 = few credible sellers chasing this problem; 1 = saturated.

**Founder credibility is capped at 4, by construction.** The external-evidence standard established in P1.2 §1.11 applies unchanged: no case studies, no named references, no testimonials, no speaking record, no publications, no independently verified certifications exist anywhere in the profile (FACT, Block G29). No problem can therefore score 5. A 4 means "deepest evidenced competence *relative to the other rows*, still employer-scoped and unverified by any third party." Both lifetime Linkgurus clients bought the founder, not the firm, at minimum charge (FACT, Block A1).

**Extrapolation marking.** Scores that go beyond direct evidence from Phases 0–5 are marked **[EXT]** in the reasoning, with the inference stated. Roughly a quarter of all scores carry the mark; the density is highest in Budget availability and Recurring revenue, because no Linkgurus transaction exists at market price to calibrate either (Block A1, Block E21–22 — FACT).

**Total is out of 70.** Fourteen dimensions, equally weighted — which they are not for any real strategy. See the two structural warnings in the reading notes: the additive form hides binding constraints that P3.1's multiplicative model correctly caught, and **market access is not one of the fourteen mandated dimensions at all**, which materially flatters two rows.

**Column key:** PA Pain · UR Urgency · EI Economic impact · FR Frequency · BU Budget availability · BC Buyer clarity · EA Existing alternatives *(inv.)* · CI Competitive intensity *(inv.)* · DP Differentiation potential · FC Founder credibility · AT AI tailwind · IP Ability to create IP · PZ Ability to productize · RR Potential recurring revenue.

### A — Nitaqat quota-versus-automation job architecture · Rank 1 (50/70)

| UR | **5** | The only problem in this list of twenty with a statutory calendar the buyer does not control: Mutawar effective 26 Apr 2026; Qiwa contract authentication from 15 Apr 2026; Yellow band eliminated so borderline firms drop directly to Red (MHRSD via P2.1, P4.1 §6 — FACT). |
| EI | 4 | Band downgrade → visa/permit restrictions → operational disruption; wage floors raised (SAR 4,000 base, 8,000 engineering, 9,000 dentistry). Quantifiable, mid-size versus transformation programs. |
| FR | **5** | 269 profession-level quotas, 340,000+ jobs targeted, on annual cycles through 2028. Recurring by regulatory construction. |
| BU | 3 | Compliance spend is non-discretionary (FACT), but the money currently flows to the paperwork tier. **[EXT: ticket size inferred from category norms — no Linkgurus transaction exists to calibrate it; same assumption flagged at P2.2 row 2 BA-3 and P3.1 rank 1 B-3.]** |

## Reading notes

### Structural Warning 1 — the additive total hides binding constraints
Fourteen equally weighted dimensions cannot model a gate. P3.1 used a *multiplicative* composite precisely so that a single 1 would collapse a row, and that model correctly buried the two highest-budget segments in the market. This table's additive form does the opposite. Rows **I** (44) and **R** (38) score respectably while remaining, on the same evidence, unreachable. Rows **F** (buyer clarity 2 — burned buyers cannot be identified from outside) and **J** (budget 1 — consumer price point) each carry a single score that behaves like a gate rather than a weight. Read the totals as *problem attractiveness*, never as *Linkgurus's odds*.

### Structural Warning 2 — market access is not among the fourteen dimensions
The MISA local-partner requirement for professional consulting, Etimad prerequisites, and the RHQ rule are FACTs that cap what a 1–10-person Egypt-domiciled firm can reach in KSA government work regardless of demand (P2.1 §P). None of the fourteen mandated dimensions captures this. It flatters rows I and R most, and applies in weaker form to H. The unresolved legal opinion (P2.1 Rec. 2) on whether sub-SAR-1M and private-sector work escapes the RHQ mandate is still open, and it moves several rows.

### The five-way tie at 46 is informative, not a defect
D, E, F, C and G reach the same total through incompatible profiles, and each is held there by a different binding constraint:

| Row | Reaches 46 via | Held there by |
|---|---|---|
| C — scale-up | Founder credibility 4, productizability 4 | No urgency (3), no recurring form (2), unactivated network |
| D — AI decision paralysis | AI tailwind 5, differentiation 4, productizability 4 | Founder credibility 2, alternatives 2, standalone purchasability unproven |
| E — agentic redesign | AI tailwind 5, recurring 4, alternatives 4 | Urgency 2, founder credibility 2, vendor-published demand |
| F — post-restructuring failure | Pain 5, IP 4, competitive intensity 4 | Buyer clarity 2, urgency 2 — no targeting mechanism exists |
| G — pilot triage | AI tailwind 5, differentiation 4 | Budget 2 — subtraction has no budget line; and lowest evidence density in the list |

No tie-break is applied, because breaking it would require weighting the dimensions, and weighting them is a strategy choice reserved for Phases 7–8.

### Stream coverage check
- **Internal capability** (P1.1, P1.2, Blocks A–G): rows B, C, F, J, L, M, P — seven rows, including the two highest founder-credibility scores in the table (B, J) and the highest IP score (F).
- **Customer segments** (P3.1, P3.2): rows A, C, D, E, F, H, I, J, O, P, Q, R, S, T — fourteen rows, spanning P3.1's ranks 1 through 17 including its bottom four.

Every row draws from at least two streams; the average is three.

### Hypothesis A, scored to the same standard
The facilitated-decision-design thesis appears at four points in this table, and was neither protected nor penalized:

- **D (rank 3=, 46)** — the flagship, at its strongest available formulation. It reaches the top tie on AI tailwind, differentiation potential and productizability, and is held there by founder credibility 2, existing alternatives 2 (DSA has *repeat, paid* Gulf clients) and competitive intensity 3, which is a deliberate downgrade from P2.2's CO-4 on new Phase 5.1 evidence.
- **F (rank 3=, 46)** — the decision-and-adoption layer diagnosis, which is the thesis's strongest *evidential* leg and its weakest *commercial* one (buyer clarity 2).
- **N (rank 11=, 40)** — the form the market actually buys (capability transfer), where the founder scores 1 on credibility and the offering is blocked until the method is documented.
- **T (rank 20, 33)** — the segment where the Arabic facilitation supply is confirmed present, at High confidence.

Two rows (N, T) exist specifically because the evidence cuts against the thesis, and both were scored, not softened. Three of the four are gated by the same two unresolved items that have been open since Phase 2.1: the 8–10 buyer interviews, and the Block G28 boardroom-language clarification.

### What would most re-rank this table
1. **The 8–10 buyer interviews** (P2.1 Rec. 1). They test D's load-bearing assumption directly, and P3.2 defines the discriminating question: whether buyers describe a *knowledge* gap (which kills the facilitation thesis and favours B, L), an *alignment* gap, or a *defensibility* gap. Design them to falsify, not confirm — P3.2 flags that five of seven real-job hypotheses converged on the analyst's own prior.
2. **One promotion to the 2,500-person list.** The cheapest experiment available anywhere in this engagement; it prices J's budget dimension with data and reveals whether the audience contains budget holders or individual practitioners — an order-of-magnitude difference.
3. **The GISCON outcome, quantified with client confirmation.** The only named engagement in the profile with a blank result. It moves founder credibility on B, C, F and H simultaneously.
4. **Provenance of Shadow Mapping, Org Dysfunction Mapping and Decision Rights Mapping.** Instruments or labels — this determines whether the IP scores on D, F, L, N and O are real or aspirational.
5. **Block G28, one line.** It moves S, and the Arabic legs of A, D and O.
6. **Activating any of the five named GCC contacts** (zero approached). It moves accessibility on A, C and O — a dimension this table does not even contain.
